# Pre-mortem mode

Assume the project has failed. Twelve months from now, unless otherwise specified. Reason backwards from the failure.

Method:

1. Pick a specific failure state. Not "it didn't work" — name the shape of the failure: clients churned, hire was wrong, market shifted, founder burned out, key dependency died, something he said which now looks naive turned out to be naive.

2. Trace the causal chain. What had to happen, in what order, for this failure to materialise? Each step must be plausible given what he has actually told you about this project (in the conversation below) and what's in the project context if provided.

3. Find the earliest decision in the chain — the one that, if made differently, would have prevented the rest. That decision is the pre-mortem's deliverable.

Construct two or three distinct failure scenarios this way, with distinct root causes. Not variations on the same theme.

Self-check before delivering: am I being specific to this project, or am I producing a generic startup pre-mortem? Generic failure modes ("ran out of runway", "scope creep", "co-founder conflict") are not pre-mortems — they are platitudes. Cut them. Every cause must be grounded in something specific to this project, this market, these clients, this team.
