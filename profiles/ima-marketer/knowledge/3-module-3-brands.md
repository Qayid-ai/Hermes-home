### 3. Brands and their objectives

*video 23m43s · Skool-hosted video · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/caa667bf?md=913d5c84c0fb4554b449400f638ad76b) · transcript: `transcripts/m3-03-3-brands-and-their-objectives.txt`*

---

This training is designed to give you a complete understanding of how brands think, what their goals are, and how you can align your influencer marketing strategies to help them achieve their objectives.

By understanding these elements, you can pitch better, close deals faster, and build long-term relationships with brands.

---

#### 1. Marketing Objectives

The first step in working with brands is to understand **what they’re trying to achieve.**

Brands invest in influencer marketing to accomplish specific goals, and your job is to align your pitch and strategy with those objectives.

##### Primary Marketing Objectives:

- **Brand Awareness:**
    - The goal is to get the brand seen by as many people as possible.
    - **Key Performance Indicators:** Impressions, reach, video views, engagement rates (likes, comments, shares).
    - **Example:** A new brand entering the market may want an influencer to post unboxing videos or testimonials to introduce the product.
- **Lead Generation:**
    - The goal is to drive people to sign up, subscribe, or express interest in the product.
    - **KPIs:** Clicks, form fills, website traffic, email signups.
    - **Example:** A software company might collaborate with an influencer to promote a free trial.
- **Sales Conversion:**
    - The goal is direct sales or revenue generation.
    - **KPIs:** Conversions, ROI, purchase volume, affiliate sales.
    - **Example:** An e-commerce store might partner with an influencer to run a discount code campaign.
- **Product Launch:**
    - The goal is to build excitement and visibility for a new product.
    - **KPIs:** Impressions, mentions, user-generated content (UGC).
    - **Example:** A beauty brand launching a new foundation might want influencers to test it and share reviews.
- **Market Testing:**
    - The goal is to gather feedback from a specific audience.
    - **KPIs:** Sentiment analysis, comments, surveys, qualitative feedback.
    - **Example:** A new energy drink brand may test different influencer demographics to see who resonates most with the product.

---

##### Key Players in the Decision-Making Process:

- **Marketing Manager:**
    - Typically manages the campaign’s budget and execution. They often have monthly KPIs to hit and will see you as a partner to help them achieve those goals.
    - **Your Goal:** Position yourself as someone who makes their job easier by delivering results.
- **Brand Manager:**
    - Focuses on maintaining the brand’s image, messaging, and overall reputation. They care about consistency and alignment with the brand’s tone.
    - **Your Goal:** Highlight how your influencer aligns perfectly with the brand’s voice and image.
- **PR Representative (Public Relations) :**
    - Handles public relations and media collaborations. They may prioritize brand awareness over conversions.
    - **Your Goal:** Pitch campaigns that generate buzz and attention.
- **Founder / CEO :**
    - Main decisions makers in the company
    - **Your Goal:** Make them feel understood and be their hero.

---

##### How Decisions Are Made:

- **Budget Allocations:**
    Marketing managers typically have monthly or quarterly budgets to spend. Their job is to allocate this budget effectively to hit their KPIs.
    - **Example:** If they have $50k left for Q4 and want to sellout 1000 products with this, your job is to show them how a $10k influencer campaign can help achieve at least 20% of that if not more, for it to make a lot of sense for them.
    - You don't have to dive into the details of the numbers, but make sure to show them the ROI potential is heavy and secured.
- **Who Holds Buying Power:**
    - Smaller brands: **The CEO** or **Founder** often makes the final decision.
    - Larger brands: **The marketing manager** has autonomy within their budget but may need approval for bigger spends from higher-ups.

---

#### 2. Types of Campaigns

Not all campaigns are created equal. The type of campaign a brand is running will dictate their goals, expectations, and budget.

##### Product Launch Campaigns

- **Objective:** Build hype and awareness for a new product.
- **Strategy:** Use influencers to showcase the product in creative, engaging ways that generate curiosity and excitement.
- **Example:** A fitness brand launching a new supplement could ask influencers to create "first impression" videos and share workout tips featuring the product.

---

##### Long-term Ambassador Programs

- **Objective:** Build a consistent brand presence through ongoing collaborations with influencers.
- **Strategy:** Partner with influencers who truly align with the brand’s values and audience, and have them represent the brand over months or years.
- **Example:** A fashion brand might have an influencer post about their clothing once a month, with a focus on authenticity and storytelling.

---

##### Seasonal/Short-term Promotions

- **Objective:** Drive sales or engagement during a specific period, like Black Friday or holiday seasons.
- **Strategy:** Time-sensitive offers, discount codes, and urgent calls-to-action.
- **Example:** A skincare brand could have influencers promote a holiday bundle deal with a discount code valid for 48 hours.

---

##### Awareness vs. Conversion-focused Campaigns

- **Awareness Campaigns:**
    - Focus on reach, impressions, and engagement.
    - **Example:** A startup promoting its app may want an influencer to show how the app fits into their daily routine.
- **Conversion Campaigns:**
    - Focus on driving measurable actions like sales or signups.
    - **Example:** A brand running a conversion campaign with a trackable discount code. The goal is direct Return On Investment.

---

#### 3. Brand Messaging and Image

Brands care deeply about how they’re perceived. Consistency in messaging and tone is key.

##### Understanding Brand Messaging:

- **Consistency is Crucial:**
    - Brands want to maintain the same image across all campaigns. If they’re playful and fun, they’ll want the influencer’s tone to match that.
- **Adapting the Influencer’s Content:**
    - Help influencers adjust their messaging to align with the brand’s image while maintaining authenticity.
    - **Example:** A luxury skincare brand may prefer polished, elegant content, while a streetwear brand may want something edgy and casual.

---

##### Brand Tone:

- **Examples of Tones:**
    - Playful: Use humor, memes, and relatable content.
          - **Example:** An influencer promoting a casual snack brand might create a funny skit about late-night cravings.
    - Professional: Focus on expertise and authority.
          - **Example:** A tech influencer reviewing a B2B software solution might use stats, graphs, and formal language.
    - Inspirational: Motivate and inspire the audience.
          - **Example:** A fitness influencer working with a health supplement brand might share a before-and-after story.

---

#### 4. Types of Deals

Understanding the types of deals brands might offer is essential. Here’s how to navigate them:

##### Flat Fee Deals:

- **What It Is:** The brand pays a fixed amount for the influencer’s content.
- **When to Use:** Best for clear deliverables like Youtube Integrations, Instagram posts or TikToks.
- **Example:** *“The brands pays the influencer $3,000 for a sponsored YouTube video featuring their product.”*

---

##### CPM (Cost Per Thousand Impressions) Deals:

- **What It Is:** Payment is based on the number of impressions the influencer generates.
- **When to Use:** Ideal for awareness campaigns (and safer on the brands side).
- **Example:** *“We’ll pay $20 per 1,000 views on your next Youtube Video, we will cap it at $5000 and count the views for the next 30 days.” You can negotiate to remove the cap or increase it, as well as negotiate an extension of monitoring period to 90 days. You can even negotiate for more, it just depends on your aikido skills and the brand's flexibility. *

---

##### As An Agency, We Try and Avoid Affiliate Deals:

- **What It Is:** The influencer earns a percentage of sales or a flat fee per conversion.
- **Challenges:** Requires trust in the brand and clear tracking mechanisms.
- **Example:** *“You’ll earn $10 per sale generated through your unique discount code.”*

---

##### If The Brands Really Insist, We Offer Hybrid Deals:

- **What It Is:** A combination of flat fees and affiliate commissions.
- **When to Use:** When brands want to minimize upfront costs but still incentivize performance.
- **Example:** *“We’ll pay $1,500 upfront and offer 10% commission on all sales.” Tracked using discount codes and links in the description. You can share both the upfront revenue and the commission with the influencer. *

---

##### We Avoid Barter/Free Product Deals (Unless it's a mad expensive product):

- **What It Is:** The influencer is compensated with free products instead of money.
- **Challenges:** Not ideal for professional influencers, but may work for micro-influencers or those just starting out.
- **Example:** *“We’ll send you our $500O bed set in exchange for one Instagram post.”*

---

#### 5. How to Approach and Impress Brands

##### Position Yourself as a Problem-Solver:

- Highlight how you help brands hit their KPIs with precision. (influencers audience aligning with the brand's objective, high engagement, high trust etc)
- Show them you understand their objectives and can tailor your approach to them specifically. Make them an offer that makes sense.

##### Tailor Your Pitch:

- Mention specific campaign ideas and how they align with their goals.
    - **Example:** *“We’ve got MrBeast planning a detailed unboxing video for your new product launch. It’s the perfect way to build excitement and drive engagement. before your Black Friday offer !”*

##### Offer Add-Ons:

- If the brand has a smaller budget, propose bonus content or guarantees.
    - **Example:** *“To maximize ROI, we can include an Instagram story with a guaranteed 100,000 views at no additional cost.”*

---

##### By understanding brands’ objectives and tailoring your strategies accordingly, you’ll be able to build stronger relationships, close more deals, and position yourself as an invaluable partner in their marketing efforts.

#### How to Find High-Paying Brands for Influencer Marketing

1. **Understanding the Importance of High-Paying Brands**
    - Target brands that are already investing in influencer marketing.
    - Avoid having to explain influencer marketing to them.
    - Focus on brands that are already spending money on influencers.
2. **Initial Steps**
    - Look at the big influencers in your niche, and who is sponsoring them.
    - Use your tracking sheet to review also check these influencers.
    - Open up the past 6 months - 1 year of content or the last 50-100 pieces of content from each influencer to identify sponsorships.
    - Note down the brands they are working with, regardless of whether you have a deal with the influencer or not.
3. **Storing data on High-Paying Brands**
    - Note down the brand names and influencers involved.
    - Personalize your outreach by mentioning the influencer's partnership with the brand.
4. **Locating Contact Information**
    - Google the brand name along with "LinkedIn" to find company profiles.
    - Write down 'Marketing' or 'Influencer' next to the name, to find the right employee in that company.
    - Use [**Apollo**](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/apolloio-free-b2b-phone-n/alhgpfoeiimagjlnfekdhkjlkiomcapa) to scrap the email from the qualified employees.
    - If you can't find the direct person to reach out to, reach out to other employees, starting from the highest hierarchy to the lowest.
    - If you're going very deep with employees unrelated to this field of work, ask them to redirect you to the right people.
    - Do not copy and paste messages to different employees, for each time, let them know you're reaching out because you didn't know who was the right person/you couldn't get in touch with them and kindly ask them to guide you to the right person.
    - Use Apollo's [free extension](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/apolloio-free-b2b-phone-n/alhgpfoeiimagjlnfekdhkjlkiomcapa) to find the email on the Linkedin profiles.
5. **Professional Outreach**
    - Reach out to marketing personnel in a professional manner.
    - Be very resilient and sticky with the followup, one brand can change your life.
    - Follow up politely to show continued interest.
6. **Building Relationships**
    - Approach brand outreach with a relationship-building mindset.
    - (Not a money milking mindset)
    - Maintain professionalism and persistence without being overly aggressive.

### 5. The foot in the door method

*video 17m01s · Skool-hosted video · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/caa667bf?md=767b73ffc83d48f9a2d58a326ab77571) · transcript: `transcripts/m3-11-5-the-foot-in-the-door-method.txt`*

**Brands don’t know you or your influencer**.

That’s why you can’t go in expecting a big deal right away.

You need to start small, prove you know your stuff, and then **make your money in the upsells**.

1 good brand that trusts you can change your life.

It’s all about getting a foot in the door, building trust, and scaling up from there.

Here’s exactly how you do it:

---

##### A. Start with a Small Offer – “Foot in the Door” Method

Don’t pitch a huge package or campaign when you just met them.

Brands don’t know you yet, so you need to **start small** to get them comfortable.

The goal here is simple: **show them results quickly** and build trust so they’re open to spending more later.

###### 1. Offer a Single Test Integration:

- Begin with **one video** or **one integration**. It’s low-risk for the brand and gives you a chance to show what you can deliver.
- Once they see good results, upsell them to a **bigger package** like a **3-5 video deal**, or even a multi-influencer campaign. (but don't talk about this until the first video is out ! otherwise you'll come off as a greedy middle man just here to milk them for their money !)

###### 2. Why This Works:

- Brands are more likely to take a small risk before committing big money. This way, they see your value without feeling pressured.
- After they trust you and see results, it’s way easier to pitch bigger deals and lock in **long-term contracts**.

---

##### B. The First Deal MUST Perform

If you want to make real money, the first campaign **has to deliver**.

Mess it up, and you won’t get the chance to upsell. That's why I told you to pick influencers with good engagement, from first world countries etc. The goal is that you get the brand RESULTS so that they trust you.

**Key Rule : Pick the Right Influencer for the Job:**

- Don’t just shoot your list of influencers to them—make sure the influencer you choose **fits perfectly** with the brand’s target audience. Make sure their content align perfectly with the brands. Also,  double-check their analytics: **location**, **age**, and **purchasing power** should match the brand’s needs.
- Avoid influencers whose audience is mainly in low-purchasing power regions unless it’s exactly what the brand wants.

---

##### C. Adjust the Offer Based on the Brand’s Budget and Vibe

Not all brands are the same. Some are **tight with budget**, while others are ready to spend big.

You have to adapt your offer based on what they’re saying and how they respond.

###### 1. If the Brand is Hesitant or Tight on Budget:

- **CPM Model**: Offer a pay-per-view/impression deal. It’s low-risk for them because they pay based on actual results. You can negotiate with your influencer to pay him a $9 CPM, and you can offer a $15 CPM to the brand. If you're getting paid on commissions from the influencer, you can directly pitch the brand for a higher CPM and try to lock in the best deal possible for both parties.
- Keep it small and simple—a single video test campaign. Show them you’re serious but willing to **build the relationship** over time.

###### 2. If the Brand is Confident and Has Money to Spend:

- **Flat fee **: Go for fixed-price fee that make things simple. Here again you can negotiate to pay your influencer a bit less than what he usually charges, and pitch the brand a higher price. You can pocket profit the difference. When you're starting out, aim to make at least $500-$1k profit per deal, but don't be greedy. If making less profit on your first deal allows you to build more trust, accept a cheaper deal with lower profit, it will be worth it in the long term.

###### 3. Adapt the Offer to Their Goal:

- If they want **brand awareness**, show them how many views your influencer can pull.
- If they want **ROI and sales**, present your influencer’s audience as the perfect match for their product. Show them the customer base and provide past success examples to back it up. (if possible)

---

#### Summary: Steps to Securing a Deal

1. **Start Small**: Use the foot-in-the-door approach with a single test video or post. Brands need to see proof first.
2. **Make the First Campaign a Hit**: The first impression matters. Nail the results so you can upsell them easily.
3. **Adjust Your Offer**: Read the brand’s response—go small and safe with hesitant brands, and pitch bigger deals for confident ones.

Don't use any fancy words, be professional but straight to the point.

Don't be snaky about the offer, show them you can get them results, and **scale up the money** once they trust you.

That’s how you build long-term partnerships and secure the bag.

##### 1. The Basics: Framing the Conversation to Pre-Handle Objections

The goal is to avoid objections before they even arise—this is called pre-objection handling.

You’re setting the tone in a way that closes the door to common issues brands raise later, saving yourself a headache.

It's like asking if you're hungry before offering you food.

###### **When reaching out : **

You’re not “just reaching out.”

You’re a professional representing exceptional influencers who deliver real results.

You’re here because you know what you’re doing and because working with you will benefit them.

*(Obviously, don’t say it in a way that sounds over the top or cringe, but that’s the vibe you need to carry in your emails: confidence, precision, and expertise.)*

**What to Say About Yourself : **

When the brand asks about you or your agency, keep it simple, subtle, and focused on showcasing expertise.

Here’s what they want to hear :

- You’ve worked with multiple brands successfully.
- You manage a few highly successful influencers.
- You specialize in their niche and understand their audience deeply.

If they don’t ask about you, don’t volunteer unnecessary details.

Always center the conversation around the influencer’s strengths and achievements instead.

Leverage their social proof—it’s a stronger trust builder than oversharing about yourself.

**What NOT to Say**

Avoid phrases or framing that sounds needy, you will lose your authority and lose control of the relationship.

Especially :

- Don’t mention lack of exclusivity.
  
  Saying something like “these influencers are interested” signals that you don’t have full control, which opens the door for the brand to demand exclusivity.
  Instead, use phrases like “we,” “he asked us to reach out,” or “we’re reaching out on his behalf” to sound more confident.
- Don’t reveal that you’re just connecting dots.
  
  If the brand feels like you’re a middleman, they may try to bypass you.

**Lead the conversation**

Instead of being reactive, take control of the narrative from the start.

Position yourself as an expert who specializes in connecting brands with the right audiences:

> "I specialize in [their niche] and help [specific type of brands, e.g., tech companies/car brands/productivity tools]  like yours tap into [specific audience demographic] audiences through trusted creators like [other influencers you’ve worked with].
> I’d love to show you how [Influencer Name] can help drive [specific outcome, e.g., engagement, sales, or awareness] to your business !"

The key is to make the brand feel like you understand their niche and that collaborating with you is a no-brainer because you’ve done this successfully before.

  Respond confidently:

##### 4. Key Mistakes to Avoid

- **Overloading Information:** Don’t include too much about yourself or your agency unless directly asked. Stick to what matters most to the brand.

- **Weak Follow-Ups:** Brands often need reminders. A follow-up email 24 hours after the first email, then 1–2 days after each email, and once every 2–3 days after your initial pitch is crucial.

If they don’t respond:

#### Part 2: The “Foot in the Door” Method

##### What is "Foot in the Door" Method

The **Foot-in-the-Door method** is a strategy where you focus on presenting **one influencer** rather than a whole list to the brand. This approach is all about **starting small** and gradually building the relationship, giving you the chance to show the brand that you've done the homework and found **the perfect fit** for them.

You’re not overwhelming the brand with options; instead, you’re highlighting **one solid influencer** who you believe will be the best for their campaign.**Why Presenting One Influencer Works Best**

###### Why is the Foot-in-the-Door Method Useful/Important?

1. **Avoids Overwhelm:**
    - Brands can get overwhelmed when you send them a long list of influencers. Too many options mean too many decisions, and **decision fatigue** can cause them to pass on all options.
    ---
2. **Makes You Stand Out:**
    - If you present **one carefully chosen influencer**, you appear more strategic. It shows you're focused and not just tossing names out there for the sake of filling quotas. **You’re providing value**, not just options.
          ---
3. **Easier for Brands to Say "Yes":**
    - When you present **one perfect influencer**, it's easier for the brand to say yes because they don’t have to weigh multiple choices. It makes their decision process **clearer and simpler**.

    - By showing that you've taken the time to understand the brand's goals and audience, you build **trust**. Brands start to see you as a partner who cares about aligning their needs with the right talent.

### 4. How to Negotiate with Brands

Negotiating with brands isn’t about throwing prices and hoping something sticks—it’s about understanding their perspective, presenting real value, and securing the best deal for your influencer.

#### Here’s how to structure your approach step by step:

###### 1. Anchor High

- Always **start with a higher price** than what you’re willing to accept.
    - If you’ve already negotiated the influencer's rate down, **pitch their original price or even slightly higher** to give yourself room to adjust.
    - Never pitch below the influencer’s asking price unless it’s *absurdly overpriced*.

Regardless, even if you got him down to $2k. You can always let the brand know you can work on the price if the opportunity makes sense (without being needy ofc)
          > - Overpriced Example : If your influencer asks $30k for a collaboration but realistically their market value is closer to $5k, and you got him down to $3k.  Pitch him for to the brands for $8k. Then see with the brand where they can settle at.

###### 2. Show the Good Angles (Make it Make Sense for Them)

- Before pitching, ask yourself:
    - **Why would they buy this?**
    - **How does this collaboration make sense for their goals?**
- Example:
    > If the brand sells keyboards, think:
    - Has the influencer promoted a keyboard recently?
          - (If not, it’s fresh and they're ready to buy ✅)
          - (If yes, you can showcase how good it went ✅)
    - Does the influencer’s audience match the target demographic for keyboards?
          - If yes, show them !
          - If not, show how this could still make sense (aikido) Obviously don't lie tho, don't start trying to sell ice cream in Alaska and saying this would be game changer, you'll look stupid.
    - Is their audience actively engaging with tech-related posts?
          - This one has to be yes, just emphasize on how good the engagement is and on how much ROI it'll result on the brand's side.
- Focus on positioning the collaboration as a **win-win** for the brand.
    - Example: If the influencer's audience loves productivity tools and the brand sells ergonomic keyboards, highlight how this partnership aligns perfectly.
- Don’t stop at just one angle—be creative. For Example:
    > If the influencer recently switched to a standing desk, pitching an ergonomic keyboard fits the narrative and gives the audience a seamless next step.

---

###### 3. Refer to the Influencer Initial Pricing—but Always Aim Higher

- **Start High, Come Down Later:**
    - Influencer pricing always leaves wiggle room. Don’t pitch a low price to “be safe.” Start at the higher end of the spectrum and adjust if necessary.
- **Broke Company? Adjust Without Needing Discounts:**
    - If you suspect the company doesn’t have a big budget, avoid looking desperate by pitching discounts upfront. Instead, position yourself as flexible:
          > “We’d love to explore ways to make this work. If the opportunity aligns well, we can see how to create a win-win arrangement.”

###### 4. If Brands Want Cheaper Deals (Not Because They're Broke, but cheaper per Unit for ROI)

- Sometimes brands aren’t broke but hesitant about ROI (Return on Investment). In such cases:
    - Offer **bundles** to sweeten the deal.

##### 1. Understanding Pre-Objection Handling

Pre-objection handling means addressing potential concerns *before* they arise, so the brand doesn’t even have the chance to object in the first place. This isn't about manipulation; it’s about being proactive and making it easy for the brand to make a decision without unnecessary back-and-forth.

When reaching out to brands, the goal is to make your pitch clear, efficient, and hard to ignore. By handling objections early, you avoid spending weeks on brands that were never going to convert. It’s better to know in the first email if this is even a real opportunity.

We will also show you that, by handling a concern before the price drop, it's not really an objection. They can still change their mind. But if it's after price drop, its a mess. And now it seems like you're trying to convince them.

Pre handling an objection is basically handling an objection before it actually becomes an objection. Imagine someone asking you if you're hungry before offering to sell you food, instead of him trying to convince you that you're hungry once he pitched you his special shawarma offer.

Also called, Aikido :)

##### 2. The Importance of Early Qualification

##### 3. Replacing Trigger words with Better Language

Brands can get defensive when you ask about their budget directly. Words like "budget" feel like a setup to get all their money. Instead, reframe the conversation to focus on value and ease for them.

- **Avoid the Word "Budget":**
    - **Bad Phrasing:**
          - "What’s your budget for this?"
          - "How much are you willing to spend?"
    - **Better Phrasing:**
          - "What resources can you allocate for this campaign?"
          - "What spending range are you comfortable with?"
          - "We’ve got influencers ranging from $1k to $20k. Should we include influencers at the higher range, or would you like us to cap it at $5k?"
          - "Should I include influencers with higher fees, or do you prefer to stay below a certain price point?"
          - "To make it easier for you, we can narrow down the list based on the price range you’re comfortable with. What range usually works for you?"

By making it feel like you're helping them avoid hassle, they’re more likely to respond positively. This framing doesn’t sound greedy or pushy. You show that you care about *their* preferences and making things easy for them. It forces them to give at least a general price range without putting you in a “guessing” position.

##### 4. Sending Fewer Influencers, Not Long Lists

When brands ask for a list of influencers, don't send them 5–10 names upfront. Here's why:

1. **Overwhelm:** A long list makes it seem like you don’t know what they’re looking for.
2. **Risk of Rejection:** If they don’t like any from the list, they might ghost you completely.
3. **Perceived Expense:** A big list can feel like a massive financial commitment (e.g., "Wow, this is going to cost $50k, let’s just ignore this").

**Golden Rule:** Always start small. Sending one influencer at a time is safer than overwhelming them with a massive list, especially if the prices are high.

- **Why?**
    - Sending five influencers at once increases the chance that they’ll dismiss all of them if even one doesn’t align with their expectations.
    - By sending one influencer at a time, you can adjust your approach based on their feedback before pitching others.

Instead, start with **one highly relevant influencer**. Use a **"foot in the door"** approach:

> *"We’ve got a few influencers who could be a great fit, but here’s one who I think matches your goals perfectly. If this aligns with what you’re looking for, I can share another option."*

This way, you minimize risk and keep the conversation moving forward.

##### 5. Qualifying Brands Without Asking "What’s Your Budget?"

Brands often avoid giving an exact budget because they don’t want to overcommit. Instead of asking, "What’s your budget?" (which can sound greedy), frame it differently:

- **Example 1:**
    > *"We work with influencers across a wide range of pricing, from $1k to $20k. Should I include options above $20k, or would that be outside what you’re looking for?"*
- **Example 2:**
    > *"We have influencers who deliver incredible results, but their rates vary based on the campaign. What do you typically invest in campaigns like this, so I can match you with the right talent?"*
- **Example 3:**
    > *"We’ve got a few great influencers we think you’d love. Would you prefer to see those in the $5-10k range, or should we include some at a higher range as well?"*

This way, you're making the conversation about **helping them**, not just extracting money. You’re not asking for a budget to milk their money—you’re trying to avoid wasting their time with irrelevant influencers. Frame it this way to come off as helpful and professional.

##### 6. Why This Approach Matters

When you ask the right questions and pre-handle objections, it does two things:

1. It **qualifies the brand** so you don’t waste time pitching influencers they can’t afford.
2. It **removes price as an objection later** because you’ve already ensured the influencers you present align with their spending capacity.

- **Never send a massive influencer list upfront**—start with one or two to gauge their response.

By qualifying brands early and pre-handling objections, you’re not just increasing your chances of closing deals—you’re saving yourself from wasting time on brands that were never going to say yes in the first place.

          - For example, he wants 50% payment upfront.
          - Go back to the brand, and ask them to update the contract to include 50% upfront payment.
          - If they accept, they will send you the updated contract, dupplicate it again and forward it to the influencer, repeating the same process.
          - If they don't accept, forward the content of their answer to the influencer and explain why this change can't be done.
          - You are the middle man, you stay in the middle all the time, and you forward each request to the other party.
    5. **Sign and Finalize:**
          - Once the influencer agrees, have them sign the contract.
          - After that, sign the contract with the brand.
          - **YOU NEVER SIGN FIRST. THE INFLUENCER SIGNS FIRST, YOU SIGN WITH THE BRAND AFTER !  **

##### Step 1: Picking and Setting Up Your Payment Accounts

> ##### Quick Tip:
> - **Start with PayPal** when you’re closing your first few deals — it’s fast and easy for everyone.
> - **Switch to Wise or Revolut** when you start closing bigger deals ($500+ and up) to **save money on transaction and currency conversion fees**.

Before sending invoices, make sure you have reliable ways to collect money. I recommend to have all 3 to give diversity and options to brands and cater to different brands depending on which region they are in. they all have their unique benefits and reason to use them.

---

###### 1. PayPal

**Why PayPal?**
PayPal is the easiest and fastest way to get paid, especially when you’re first starting out.
Almost every brand is familiar with it, and setting it up is quick.

**How to set up PayPal:**

1. Go to [paypal.com](http://paypal.com).
2. Click **Sign Up** and choose **Business Account**.
3. Enter your name, email, and create a password.
4. Confirm your email address.
5. Link your **debit card** (or bank account if available in your country).
6. Set your **default currency** to **USD** (or GBP/EUR depending on your clients).
7. Enable **two-factor authentication** for extra security.

> **Important:** Only share your **PayPal email address** with brands — never your card or banking info.

---

###### 2. Revolut

**Why Revolut?**
Revolut is great for getting paid by **direct bank transfer** — especially from brands in Europe or the UK.
It offers multi-currency accounts with **low fees** and **better exchange rates** than PayPal.

**How to set up Revolut:**

1. Download the **Revolut app**.
2. Sign up and **verify your identity** (upload your ID or passport).
3. Open a **multi-currency account** (USD, GBP, EUR).
4. Get your personal **account numbers and routing numbers** inside the app.
5. Share these details when brands ask to pay you by bank transfer.

> **Important:** Revolut makes it easier and cheaper to collect larger payments without paying high PayPal fees.

---

###### 3. Wise (formerly TransferWise)

**Why Wise?**
Wise is the best choice for **international payments**.
It gives you real bank details (like a US bank account) even if you live outside the US, plus **low fees** and **honest currency exchange rates**.

**How to set up Wise:**

1. Go to [wise.com](http://wise.com) and sign up.
2. Verify your identity.
3. Open a **multi-currency account**.
4. Get your **local bank details** for USD, GBP, EUR, and more.
5. Share these bank details with brands when they prefer wire transfers.

> **Important:** Wise is ideal for bigger deals or international clients because it keeps more money in your pocket instead of wasting it on bad currency rates.

#### Step 3: Send the Invoice to the Brand

Now that your invoice is ready, the next step is to send it the right way.

> ##### PRO TIP: Always send your invoice in PDF format

###### Why You Always Send Invoices as a PDF

**Professionalism:**
A PDF looks clean and official. It shows the brand you take your business seriously.

**Lock the Information:**
When you send a Word Doc, Google Doc, or Canva file, someone can accidentally (or intentionally) **edit your invoice**.
A PDF protects your original invoice — no one can change the amount, due date, or payment info.

**Consistency:**
PDFs look the same on every device (phone, tablet, computer), while Word or Google Docs might display differently depending on who’s opening it.

**Easier for Brands:**
Most finance departments and accounting software are set up to **only accept invoices in PDF format**.
Sending a Word file can cause delays because they’ll have to convert it themselves (and they won't want to).

##### In this training, we will break down the post-campaign process step by step.

> 1. You’ll learn how to collect and analyze data from your campaigns,
> 2. Ensure the brand is satisfied
> 3. How to handle situations when results don’t meet expectations.

Proper post-campaign management will not only help you deliver excellent service but also build your relationships with both brands and influencers.

---

#### Step 1: Collect and Track Data

After the influencer’s content has gone live, it’s essential to measure the campaign's performance. This will allow you to evaluate the effectiveness of the content and the influencer’s reach, as well as provide valuable insights for future campaigns.

##### Action Items:

1. **Ask the Influencer for Analytics:**
    - Reach out to the influencer and request data on the campaign. This includes key metrics such as:
          - **Engagement rate** (likes, comments, shares)
          - **Impressions and reach**
          - **Video views** (if applicable)
          - **Click-through rates** (if you included links or CTAs in the post)
    - Make sure to ask for these analytics in a format that is easy to share and interpret (e.g., screenshots, Excel files, etc.).
    ---
2. **Forward Data to the Brand:**
    - Once you receive the analytics, forward this data to the brand, especially if the results are positive.
    - **Example:** “Here are the analytics for the campaign. As you can see, the influencer’s post garnered 1.2M impressions and a 5% engagement rate, which is above the industry average for this niche.”
    - If the results are excellent, make sure to highlight them and use this information to increase the brand’s satisfaction and trust in your agency.

---

#### Step 2: Ensure Brand Satisfaction

After the campaign, it’s important to confirm that the brand is happy with the results. Your goal is to build strong, lasting relationships with the brand, and confirming their satisfaction is a huge part of that.

##### Action Items:

1. **Confirm that the Brand is Happy:**
    - Reach out to the brand and ask for feedback on the campaign’s performance. The goal is to see whether the brand’s expectations were met, exceeded, or not met. You can phrase the question like this:
          - “How satisfied are you with the campaign results? Do you feel the influencer’s content aligned well with your goals?”
    - Always assume they are happy with the results unless they explicitly state otherwise. This approach will prevent triggering unnecessary complaints, allowing for a smoother conversation.
    ---
2. **Upsell if Results Were Positive:**
    - If the campaign results were positive, use the opportunity to upsell the brand to a bigger or recurring deal. For example:
          - “We’ve seen great results with this campaign. I’d love to discuss a larger campaign or recurring partnership for the next quarter. What do you think about working together again?”
    ---
3. **Handle Negative Results Effectively:**
    - If the results weren’t as expected, don’t panic. You can take action to resolve the situation and keep the relationship intact.
    - **Important:** Handle bad results proactively and with confidence, as this is a chance to show your professionalism.

---

#### Step 3: Handling Bad Results: Step-by-Step

If the results of the campaign aren’t what the brand hoped for, here’s how to professionally handle the situation and minimize damage.

##### Action Items:

1. **Acknowledge the Mistake:**
    - Begin by acknowledging the brand’s concerns. This is your chance to show that you understand their frustration and are taking the issue seriously.
    - **Example Response:**
          - “I completely understand your concern. It seems like the campaign didn’t perform as expected, and I want to ensure we resolve this to your satisfaction.”
    ---
2. **Reassure the Brand (If Applicable):**
    - If the situation allows, try to reassure the brand that the issue might not be as severe as it seems. Explain why this might work in their favor:
          - **Example:** “While the engagement may be lower than expected, this content could stand out in a more organic way compared to the typical polished ads. It may also improve over time as the content gains more traction.”
    - **Important:** Only use this tactic if it’s valid and truthful. If the mistake significantly affects the brand’s sales or goals, do not try to spin it as a positive. Be transparent.
    ---
3. **Find an Effective Compensation Plan:**
    - Offer a solution that addresses the issue. Rather than simply redoing the entire campaign, offer alternative solutions that can help mitigate the impact.
    - **Example Solutions:**
          - If the video had a technical issue (like poor lighting or sound), you could offer an additional Instagram story or a bonus post to make up for it.
          - If sales dropped by 20% due to an editing mistake, you could arrange for an additional post or shoutout to compensate for the lost impact.
          - **Alternative Compensation**: Offering something valuable without fully redoing the campaign. This could include a secondary post or a series of stories to drive more attention.
    ---
4. **Set Clear Boundaries:**
    - It’s important to set expectations regarding what’s possible and what isn’t. A completely free redo of the campaign might not be feasible, especially if it involves costs like influencer fees, production costs, and time.
    - **Example:** “I understand that the campaign didn’t go as planned, but I want to be transparent. Due to production costs, we’re unable to redo the entire ad for free. However, I’ve come up with a solution that I believe will make up for it.”
    ---
5. **Apologize (Without Neediness):**
    - Offer a professional apology for the oversight or issue, but do not apologize in a way that undermines your authority or creates a sense of desperation.
    - **Example:** “I apologize for the oversight in this campaign. I completely understand the impact and want to make sure we correct this swiftly. Let’s move forward with the best solution possible for your brand.”

---

###### Action Steps:

> - **Acknowledge** the brand’s concerns and show that you take the issue seriously.
> - **Reassure** the brand (if applicable) by explaining how the situation could still work in their favor.
> - **Offer compensation** that provides value without completely redoing the campaign.
> - **Set clear boundaries** about what’s possible to fix and why.
> - **Apologize professionally** while maintaining confidence and solution-orientation.

By following these steps, you make sure that you handle bad campaign results with professionalism. You’ll protect the brand relationship, maintain your credibility, and keep the influencer accountable while negotiating fair solutions.
