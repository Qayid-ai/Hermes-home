### 1. Setting expectations

*video 29m42s · Skool-hosted video · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/8be30aa5?md=0eb6811f21e24716a5218a8eccefffc6) · transcript: `transcripts/m2-01-1-setting-expectations.txt`*

#### Setting Expectations for the Influencer Section

Here, you'll learn everything about outreach, how to find influencers, message them, pricing them, negotiating and so on.

But before we dive in, I want to set some expectations so you know what to expect.

---

#### Don't Expect Everyone to Respond

Sometimes, I send out 50 emails and no one responds. It's the rule of averages. If you get more than 1 response every 50 emails, you're doing good.

You'll run into challenges:

- **Agents Giving You a Headache:**  Agents are tough to deal with, but you'll get used to it.
- **The Classic "What Do You Have in Mind?":** You'll get this response a lot. (use aikido)
- **Influencers Thinking You're a Brand:** They'll often assume you're a brand at first. Need to aikido your way here.
- **They Won't Know You're an Agency:** Depending on if they have an agent, you'll decide how to proceed. Sometimes using a smokescreen can help (ex: I'm an agent representing xyz brand if you know the guy won't like agencies, or if a brand wants to work with creators directly you can say "I'm reaching out on behalf of xyz influencer (if agreed with him beforehand) etc.

---

#### How to Handle Different Situations

- **If They Have an Agent or Are Already Managed:**
    - 99% of the time, you'll go the **discount way**. Negotiate a better price and go upsell it to the brand.
- **If They Don't Have a Manager and Have Crazy Potential (Very Rare):**
    - Do **one deal with a discount** first.
    - Then consider **exclusivity** and manage them.
    - Don't jump into exclusivity right away—you never know what might happen. Better safe than sorry.

---

#### Your Goal: Send as Many Emails as Possible (while maintaining very good personalization)

- **Dig Deep:** Search through YouTube and other social media platforms to find  as many influencers as possible.
- **Get Their Audience Info:** You'll need details about their audience and age because brands will ask.
    - **Ask for Their Media Kit**
    - **Or Ask for Info on Their Audience (age % and country%)**
- **Be Normal and Honest:** Don't be sneaky or sketchy. Just be straightforward, and insha'Allah, everything will be okay. 
  
  If they have concerns before sending you over some info, make sure you really calm them and comfort them. Explain what you're here for and that you're just running your business. 
  
  Ex : You're here to get them hooked up with a brand (be specific about the brand)
    Or to help them get more partnership if they're not getting many
    Or to help them find brands that are a better fit for their content (because you're an expert at digging up brands and negotiating with them), etc.
    If you don't have experience, don't lie. Just be honest, and they will like it, you can get away by showing they won't have any risks on their end, and show them you're ready to put in whatever it takes to prove your worth. Once you got experience, simply leverage case studies to make negotiations easier.
    Get ready, because this will happen a lot. Lots of people scam influencers or steal their data info, resulting in them being very defensive when you approach them. Especially when you don't have an established agency + strong case studies, which is why you need to go above and beyond to re assure them.

---

#### Expect Them to be Scared (and deal with it)

People might say things like:

- "I don't want to respond because you don't have a website."
- "You don't have a professional domain."
- "Show me past experiences."
- "Who do you manage?"

This is normal—it's the rule of averages. With those guys, there's not much you can do right now.

- **You can stil try sme Aikido Techniques:**
    - Ask them, "If I don't have this, is it a dealbreaker for you?" Would be sad to miss out on a couple thousands $$$ over a domain.
    - Tell them your website is under construction (if it's the case)
    - That you're just onboarding influencers to prepare for a an outreach campaign with brands) etc.
    - Put a crazy guarantee to show confidence (ex : If I don't get you a deal within the next 2 weeks,  feel free to block me and (insert bad downside that you don't want to happen).
    - If it worked, then move forward and ask a bit more about their info.
    - If not, it's the rules of the game, just move forward.

You will face rejection, and it's normal.

But your goal is to **squeeze out 2-3 good influencers and get them on a nice price**, then you'll get cooking some solid brand deals insha Allah !

---

#### Understand You Fail sometimes (and it's actually good)

- **Do a Lot:** Keep sending emails.
- **It Takes Work:** Success doesn't come overnight.

**Aim to Send 30-50 Emails a Day**

- You should be able to close your first influencer in a week.
- Your second and third the next week.
- **Make Sure Emails Are Personalized and Precise**

---

#### Dealing with Overpriced Influencers

You'll come across influencers who are overpriced.

It's okay, and there's not much you can do aside from negotiating down.

If the influencer:

- Is **managed (and the agent is a headache)**
- Is **overpriced (and doesn't want to negotiate down)**
- Has an audience and engagement that **aren't impressive (and isn't dirt cheap)**

Then you can't help them.

Move on to someone else.

---

#### Motivational Tip : One Influencer Can Change Your Life

Remember, **one influencer could be enough to change your life**.

Keep that in mind. The more effort you put in now, the quicker you'll get results.

So keep pushing, keep emailing, and don't get discouraged.

Success is just around the corner, insha Allah

### 3. How do influencers get paid? CPM vs Straight

*video 9m01s · Skool-hosted video · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/8be30aa5?md=4763652de46149ae80dbe02ec375e62b) · transcript: `transcripts/m2-03-3-how-do-influencers-get-paid-cpm-vs-straight.txt`*

When making an offer, you have two ways to do it: By “**CPM**” (*Cost Per Mille*/Thousand Views)* *and directly by **Straight Deals**.

#### **CPM**:

> The cost per thousand views usually goes from 5 - 15 dollars and it depends on multiple factors, being the most influential ones: the country, niche and estimated age **of the audience.**

**A CPM deal  can look like this : **

- 10$ cpm
- 30 days monitoring period (views after 30 days don't count)
- Cap at 1m views (so you max get paid $10k, even if you make 5 million views)
- Paid 45 days after the video goes live

This is just an example, each variable can change and be negotiated with the brand.

Having in mind that you’ve picked a good niche, focus on channels which audiences are usually mature and in first world countries, like the US, Canada, Australia or the UK.

Now you understand the CPM and its ranges, but now you’re going to understand the real game.

When you’re dealing with an influencer, keep in mind that you should always try to lower his offer, and when handling the price with the brands, suggest to them to raise it for the influencer.

You may see yourself as if you are negotiating between them, but in reality you are also negotiating for yourself!

**Just Remember, DON’T sound NEEDY. **

#### Straight Deals:

This is a tool for bigger influencers but using the same principles. This time the influencer sets a fixed price for his offer and that’s it, easy as that.

**Fixed doesn’t mean unnegotiable! So no matter whatever price they offer you, do your best on the negotiation.**

#### Commission:

On both options your commission range will be around 20% - 30%, taking into account some facts like the size of the deal.

Obviously you won’t take a 60% on a 30,000$ deal, and not a 15% on a 300$ one (you can do this last thing only when starting just to make a quick portfolio and use it to obtain your first influencers).

### 2. The best way finding influencers organically

*video 14m31s · Skool-hosted video · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/8be30aa5?md=6857e1d311ef46eb83e04f827bdc1077) · transcript: `transcripts/m2-05-2-the-best-way-finding-influencers-organically.txt`*

If you’re finding that the same big names and popular channels keep popping up in your searches, you’re not alone.

YouTube's algorithm tends to push the most popular channels, making it harder to find smaller, niched-down influencers.

But with the right techniques, you can discover fresh, relevant creators organically.

##### Here’s the full strategy:

---

#### Step 1: Use Long-Tail Keywords instead of short Keywords

Instead of using generic keywords like "tech review" or "fitness vlog," get **very specific** with your searches.

YouTube favors broad, popular channels for general terms, so you have to dig deeper.

##### How to Use Long-Tail Keywords:

- Think of keywords that are very **specific** to the niche you’re targeting.
- Examples:
    - Instead of “fitness routine,” search for “beginner calisthenics workout at home” or “morning routine for busy professionals.”
    - Instead of “tech review,” search for “budget laptop review 2024” or “best tablet for note-taking 2024.”
- You can ask [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) to generate you a list of 10 Long-Tail keywords for your niche using the prompt below :
    > "Generate a list of 10 long-tail keywords for the [insert your niche] niche. The keywords should be specific and focused, targeting smaller, niche audiences rather than general terms. Make sure they are detailed enough to help find less popular but highly relevant YouTube content."

---

#### Step 2: Copy and paste Titles to find more creators

Once you find a video from a small creator in your niche, you can copy the **video title** to find other similar channels.

Here’s how:

##### Copy and Paste the Video Title:

- After watching a video that fits your niche, **copy the video title** and search it again. This will show you **other creators** making similar content, and you’ll often find smaller channels in the search results.

---

#### Step 3: Use YouTube’s Search Operators and Filters

YouTube’s search bar is actually way more powerful that you think.

The search bar works a lot like Google’s searchbar —it doesn’t just match the exact words you type in.

Instead, it uses an **algorithm** to guess what you’re looking for based on common searches and popular results. This means it often shows you what’s trending or what other users usually click when they type in similar keywords.

It also uses your own watch and search history to give you suggestions, so you’re not always seeing fresh results; you’re seeing what YouTube *thinks* you want to see.

**But there's a way to bypass this. **

To get around this and find exactly what you’re looking for, you can use **specific search operators** that refine the search results and override the algorithm’s usual patterns. This helps you bypass the usual big names and dig into smaller, niche content that’s more relevant to what you’re actually targeting.

##### A. Basic Search Operators

- **Quotes (“ ”)**: Use quotes to search for an exact phrase. For example:
    - `"morning calisthenics routine"` will return results with that exact title or phrase, giving you more precise content.

- **“Intitle:”**: Searches for specific words in the video title:
    - Example: `intitle:unboxing budget tech` will only show you videos that specifically have “unboxing” and “budget tech” in their titles.

YouTube doesn’t allow you to filter by view count directly in the UI, but you can **manipulate the URL** for specific filters:

- **Filter by Upload Date**: Add parameters like `&sp=EgIIAQ%3D%3D` at the end of your YouTube search URL to only show content uploaded in the **last month**. This is great for finding **fresh creators**.

#### Step 4: Use YouTube’s Sidebar Suggestions

YouTube’s sidebar on the right is a powerful tool that often goes overlooked.

It shows you **related content**, **similar keywords**, and **categories** based on the video you’re watching or your previous searches. This can help you discover new influencers in your niche organically.

##### A. “Based on Your Search”

- You’ll see a section labeled “Based on your search.” This is YouTube’s way of suggesting content that matches or closely relates to your current search query. It’s great for finding **more specific videos** and smaller channels that align with the type of influencer you’re looking for.

##### B. Similar Keywords & Tags

- Use the sidebar’s suggested tags and keywords to **switch up your search** without typing everything out again. This helps you quickly jump from one topic variation to another, expanding your pool of potential influencers in that niche.

#### Step 5: Reset Your Search Environment

If you find that YouTube keeps showing you the same influencers over and over, it’s because the algorithm is based on your watch history and search patterns. To get a fresh batch of results:

##### A. Clear Your Watch History and Search History

- Go to **Settings** > **History & Privacy** and clear your watch and search history. This will reset YouTube’s algorithm and give you fresh results.

##### B. Use Incognito Mode

- Use YouTube’s incognito mode or **private browsing** to search without the algorithm’s influence. This often results in new recommendations and helps you discover creators you haven’t seen before.

- Leverage **related channels** and **niche categories** to explore the deeper side of your target niche.

This method works because it goes beyond the surface-level suggestions of YouTube, digging into the algorithm’s hidden layers and using it to your advantage.

### 3. What kind of deals can be done ?

*video 24m29s · Skool-hosted video · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/8be30aa5?md=42ead0f1b18a415fa21a5aaaf0ff65a4) · transcript: `transcripts/m2-06-3-what-kind-of-deals-can-be-done.txt`*

#### There are many ways influencers get paid, but here we care about **sponsorships**.

#### We avoid affiliate deals and UGC as these don't benefit agencies.

#### We look for sponsorships, and here are the different ways of getting sponsors across each platforms :

#### On **YouTube**, you can do several things:

- **Integration:** This is a 30 to 90-second ad within the influencer's video. It's usually cheaper and gives more ROI for brands.
- **Dedicated Video:** The whole video is about the brand. Usually expensive and doesn't always give the best ROI, but it's still an option.
- **Shorts:** These are short videos, under a minute. They're cheaper and can be high risk, high reward. It's easier to get a good price because the influencer doesn't need to create an entire video, just a quick one and get the bag.
- **Community Posts:** The influencer can make a post on their community tab promoting the brand.
- **Live Streams:** The influencer mentions the brand during a live stream.

#### On **Instagram**, options include:

- **Reels:** Just like YouTube Shorts, these are short videos.
- **Stories:** Quick posts that disappear after 24 hours. Often offered as a bundle with a Reel and a link in the bio for 7 days.
- **Carousel Posts:** Multiple photos in one post. Rare but can be effective.
- **Feed Posts:** Regular photos or videos in their main profile.
- **Instagram Live:** The influencer talks about the brand during a live session.

#### On **TikTok**, the main thing is dedicated videos (60 sec):

- **Dedicated Videos:** The influencer creates a video about the brand.
- **Organic Content:** Kinda hiding the partnership within their usual content.
- **Bonus : Hashtag Challenges:** Starting a challenge sponsored by the brand.
- **Bonus : Duets or Stitches:** Interacting with the brand's content.

#### On other platforms, you can also get sponsorships:

- **Facebook:** Posts, Stories, and Live sessions.
- **LinkedIn:** Posts and articles about the brand.
- **Twitter (X):** Tweets or threads promoting the brand.
- **Emails/Newsletters:** The influencer includes the brand in their emails.
- **Community Shoutouts:** On Discord, Skool, Facebook groups, etc.
- **Podcasts:** The influencer mentions the brand in their podcast episodes.
- **Twitch:** For streamers, mentioning the brand during live streams.
- **Snapchat:** Posts or stories promoting the brand.
- **Pinterest:** For certain niches, promoting the brand through pins.
- **IRL Events:** Real-life events sponsored by the brand.

#### The influencer might offer to post across all socials for a bundle price.

#### I'm not a fan of that, as I prefer to stick to the main platform they work on.

Usually if they offer a bundle with each platform, there are 1-2 pieces of the bundle that are worth it, most of the other stuff is here to fill in your basket and increase the price they're charging, and is not worth the investment.

The list above is non exhaustive, sponsorships depend on each niche, some industry may have other ways of working with influencers, but above are the most common ones !

### How to Qualify Influencers - Coach Ammar

*video 31m37s · [video](https://vimeo.com/1179969386?share=copy&fl=sv&fe=ci) · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/8be30aa5?md=8f72a6ca889c49888dbabd7f3a70a064) · transcript: `transcripts/m2-07-how-to-qualify-influencers-coach-ammar.txt`*

Sending more emails won't fix a broken targeting process. This training shows you how to qualify influencers and brands before you pitch so the deals you close actually close.

**Influencer Qualification — 0:50**
When an influencer replies, evaluate before you pitch. Rate each creator in your Google Sheet (1–5 stars) so you always know who's reliable and who's a liability. Check these four things:

- **Average views and engagement rate.** Aim for 3–8% on smaller creators. On accounts with 1M+ views, 3% is healthy. Look for active comments — questions, reactions, real back-and-forth.
- **Posting consistency.** At least once a month with a predictable pattern. Sporadic bursts after long gaps mean it's a hobby, not a business.
- **Audience demographics.** You need at least 35% of the audience from one country. Below that, most brands won't bite regardless of the view count.
- **Responsiveness.** If it takes three days to hear back now, it'll take three days when a brand is waiting on a signed contract. Test it before you pitch them.

**Brand Lead Qualification — 16:15**
Wrong brands ghost you because you found the wrong person. For companies under 50 employees, start at the CEO and work down. For larger brands, go straight to the people with purchasing authority:

- **First tier:** Influencer Marketing Manager, Digital Marketing Manager, CMO
- **Second tier:** Influencer Specialist, Affiliate Marketing Manager
- **Third tier:** Anything marketing-related
- **Last resort:** Support, Partnerships, Info inboxes

Avoid assistants, coordinators, and anyone whose role has no budget authority.

**Using Apollo to Find the Right Contact — 25:40**
Open Apollo, pull up the brand, and go to Employees. Hit the filter icon and search by job title — start with "Influencer Marketing." Work from the top of the results down and don't skip to lower-tier contacts until you've exhausted the better ones.

**The Grand Slam Offer — 29:16**
Once you have a qualified influencer and a qualified brand, match them precisely to the brief. Before you reply, check:

- **Audience demographics** — does it meet their exact country and age requirements?
- **Niche alignment** — does the creator's content genuinely fit what the brand sells?
- **Engagement quality** — is the audience active, or are the numbers hollow?
- **Budget fit** — if their budget is $2K, don't pitch a $10K creator.
- **Brand safety** — does the creator's content conflict with anything the brand stands for?

If they give you five criteria and you hit four, you can work with that. If you're missing the main one, find a better fit first.

**Recap**
To close deals instead of sending emails, qualify at every step:

- Influencer Qualification — vet views, engagement, consistency, demographics, and responsiveness before you pitch anyone.
- Brand Lead Qualification — target decision-makers with budget authority, not gatekeepers or assistants.
- Using Apollo to Find the Right Contact — filter by job title and work from first-tier contacts down.
- The Grand Slam Offer — match the influencer to the brand's exact criteria before you reply to a single email.

### How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar

*video 28m10s · [video](https://vimeo.com/1204753838?share=copy&fl=sv&fe=ci) · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/8be30aa5?md=b0ca4a2429fe4ba4b3c126ca29b6922e) · transcript: `transcripts/m2-08-how-to-message-influencers-coach-ammar.txt`*

Most influencer emails get ignored before they're even opened. The sender's energy is wrong, the structure is off, or both. Fix those two things and you'll get replies other agency owners never see.

**Mindset — **`[0:00]`
Before writing a single word, lock in the most important rule: you are not a fanboy. You're a professional bringing value to the influencer. They need you as much as you need them. Needy energy, over-complimenting, and placing influencers on a pedestal will kill a deal before it starts. Carry the energy of someone bringing them money, because you are.

**Email Structure Overview — **`[5:07]`
Every influencer email follows exactly four parts in the same order: Subject Line, Personalization, Offer, and CTA. DMs follow a shorter version of the same structure. Skip a part or get the order wrong and the email falls apart.

**Subject Line — **`[6:37]`
The subject line is the only thing standing between your email and the trash. Influencers get dozens of "Partnership Opportunity" emails every day, and their brain is wired to skip patterns it recognises. Two approaches that cut through:

- Generic curiosity triggers. Short lines like "Thoughts" or "This might be good" create an open loop without revealing everything upfront.
- Personalized subject lines. Write your personalization first, then distil the most specific detail from it into the subject line so it feels written for that creator alone.

Never copy-paste subject lines from the internet. Train yourself to write them. The people doing that will always be one step ahead.

**Personalization — **`[10:45]`
Personalization has one job: prove you actually watched the video and wrote this email for this person specifically. If you can copy-paste it to any other influencer, it's a template. Watch 3-5 minutes of the video until you find one specific thing to comment on. Check the intro and outro for quotable moments, and look at the YouTube progress bar for spike moments where engagement peaks. Avoid AI personalization at the start. ChatGPT gives generic overviews that influencers spot immediately. Walk before you run.

**Offer — **`[16:50]`
The offer is the most important part of the email. Everything before it exists to get the influencer to this moment. If you have no brands yet, don't lie. Open 5-10 influencer profiles in your niche, list every brand they've worked with across their last 5 videos, then name 2 brands that are a strong fit for the creator you're emailing but that they haven't worked with yet. Think audience-first: who is watching this creator, and what would those people buy?

**CTA — **`[23:12]`
The CTA is the last line of your email. Its only goal is to get a reply. Keep it short, simple, and framed as a yes-or-no question. Strong options: "Are you interested?" or "What do you think about it?" Avoid "Should I send more details?" unless you actually have details ready to send.

**Full Email Example + Formatting Tips — **`[24:46]`
Target around 50 words. Short emails get read; long ones get skipped. Before sending, do the squint test: step back and look at the visual shape of your email. If it looks bulky, trim it.

- Avoid heavy whitespace and line breaks. Some providers flag it as spam.
- No fancy signatures, links, or logos. Your domain is young and you need to land in the inbox.
- Sign off with "Best regards" or "Kind regards" and your first name. Nothing more.

**Recap**
To get influencer replies, write every email with the right mindset and this four-part structure:

- Mindset — show up as a professional bringing value, not a fanboy chasing a deal.
- Email Structure Overview — follow Subject Line, Personalization, Offer, and CTA in that exact order.
- Subject Line — trigger curiosity with a short generic line or a detail pulled from your personalization.
- Personalization — prove you watched by referencing one specific, non-copy-pasteable moment.
- Offer — lead with audience fit and name real brands they haven't worked with yet.
- CTA — ask one yes-or-no question with the sole goal of getting a reply.
- Full Email Example + Formatting Tips — 50 words, clean formatting, no links, pass the squint test.

#### When you're reaching out to influencers, there are two different paths you can take (once they reply)

#### First Path: If the Influencer Already Has an Agent : Discount

If an influencer already has an agent or an email like "@[agency.com](http://agency.com)" representing them, your goal is to negotiate their price down.

This way, you can bump the price up when pitching to brands and make a profit from the difference.

#### Example :

- Influencers asked for 5k and **he is managed by an agent **
- You negotiate with his agent to get a video for 2.5k
- You go to the Brands and pitch him for 5k, making 2.5k profit. **(50% commissions).**

#### Second Path: If the Influencer Doesn't Have an Agent : Exclusive management

If an influencer doesn't have an agency and has good potential (high engagement, he just doesn't know much about partnerships etc), you can try to get him on an exclusivity deal.

That means you'll take a percentage of all the deals you bring him but also a commission from the deals that reach out to him directly (inbound).

#### Example :

- Influencers asked for 5k and he is **NOT** managed by an agent
- You offer to manage his collaborations and get more deals by **becoming his agent**
- You go to the Brands and pitch him for 5k, and you take a commission on the sale (20%-40%)

You make a bit less money on each deal,

*(Most of the time you'll make more money getting your influencers on discounts. It's easier to negotiate a 50% discount than to negotiate a 50% commission.)*

Managing an influencer has some benefits:

- **More Social Proof (Status):** Your agency looks good when you manage influencers exclusively.
- **Easier to Pitch Brands:** Brands like it when you have exclusivity.
- **More Leads:** Some brands only do outbound and handpick their influencers. So if they handpick your guy and email him, you can upsell them your other guys.

Your agency can obviously do both.

A good scenario is to always start with a discount when starting to work with an influencer.

Option 1 :

- Lock him in on exclusivity, you become his agent, you make less money per deal but you get all the other benefits.
    *You can also leverage that you can pull him way bigger deals, to get a higher commission.*

Option 2 :

- Keep him NON-EXCLUSIVE, and keep paying him his discounted rate while upselling to brands.

Pick the one that would make the most sense for you depending on the situation.

#### 2. The Core of Influencer Pricing: CPM

###### a. What is CPM?

CPM stands for **Cost Per Thousand Views**, and it’s the industry standard for influencer pricing—especially for platforms like YouTube, Instagram and Tiktok.

It allows you to assess how much it costs a brand to reach 1,000 viewers.

- **Why CPM?** It’s objective, measurable, and the simplest way to value an influencer’s audience.

---

###### b. How to Calculate CPM Pricing

The formula is straightforward,

**CPM Pricing Formula:**

![image.png](https://assets.skool.com/f/866d841bcb40438d86613587f4505c56/60ae80e36efe447eba0c1b5a1e9c09a579aeea4101e1402d9bb91d10a5391b7d)

###### Example:

> - A Tech influencer averages **100,000 views per video**.
> - His asking **price is $3000.**
> - You divide **The Price** by **The amount of views.**
> - You **Multiply it by 1000.**
> - The **CPM is $30**.
> - The **Tech niche average price is $30**, so this influencer is** fairly priced. **
> This calculation gives you a baseline price for working with the influencer.
> You’ll later negotiate to improve your margins, but for now, this is your foundation.

###### c. Typical CPM Rates by Niche

Here are some average CPM rates for different niches to guide your pricing:

- **Tech & Gadgets:** $30–$50
- **Finance (careful to avoid haram):** $40–$80
- **Health/Fitness:** $20–$35
- **Entertainment :** $10–$20
- **Lifestyle/Beauty:** $15–$25
- **Travel:** $12–$20
- **Education:** $25–$40
- **Food/Cooking:** $10–$20

#### 3. Two Ways of Paying Influencers : Flat Rate vs. CPM

###### a. Flat Rate Pricing

A **Flat Rate** is a fixed price charged by the influencer, regardless of how the campaign performs.

- **The brands bare the risk.** If the brand gets low views, the brand loses a big chunk of money at once. For influencers, it’s low risk, high reward.
- **Example:** Influencer charges $5,000 for a video. Whether the video gets 50,000 or 500,000 views, the price stays the same.

---

###### b. CPM Performance Pricing

CPM Performance payment ties the influencer’s pay to actual performance (views).

It’s fairer for brands but riskier for influencers.

- **Who Takes the Risk?** Influencers take the risk of underperformance. It’s high risk, high reward for them.
- **Example:** Influencer charges $20 CPM. If the video gets:
    - 50,000 views: $1,000
    - 100,000 views: $2,000
    - 1,000,000 views : $20,000
- **Disclaimer: **It's not a money glitch.
    - A lot of the times, influencers make less money on CPM deals.
    - The brand puts a cap on the views and the monitoring period, for example :
          - $15 CPM, capped at 200k views (max payout being $3000, you don't get paid for any views after that). The cap is negotiable with brands, but they will usually have a safety net cap to avoid going over their maximum budget per campaign.
          - 30 days monitoring period : Any views after the 30 days monitoring period won't be counted, and your payout would only be calculated on the views gathered during the 30 days monitoring window. You can request the brand to extend it to 60-90 days, but this needs to be done before securing the deal, and it must be included in the contract.

> **Pro Tip:** Use CPM pricing for brands that want to play it safe and have some trust issues with influencers, or for new partnerships where performance is uncertain.

---

###### c. When to Use Each Model

- **Flat Rate:** Ideal for trusted influencers with a strong track record. Brands accept the performance risk.
- **CPM:** Perfect for new collaborations or when the brand wants guaranteed performance.

### 5. How to Price Influencers: Step-by-Step Tutorial

*video 14m54s · Skool-hosted video · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/8be30aa5?md=e3bf5c940c3845a09a874c8c7f31d8c3) · transcript: `transcripts/m2-18-5-how-to-price-influencers-step-by-step-tutorial.txt`*

By the end of this training, you’ll not only know how to price your influencer but also have the tools to negotiate effectively while leaving room for agency profit.

##### 1. Calculate His Average Views

###### Why This Is Important:

Influencers often fluctuate in performance. One video might go viral, while another flops. To price fairly, you need a clear picture of their typical performance.

###### How to Calculate Average Views:

1. **Gather the last 12 pieces of content** (videos, posts, or stories depending on the platform).
2. **Remove the outliers**: Drop the video with the highest views and the one with the lowest views. These extremes can skew the average.
3. **Calculate the average of the remaining 10 posts** to get a realistic view count.

###### Example:

- Last 12 videos: 100k, 80k, 120k, 65k, 135k, 1M, 12k, 3k, 90k, 105k, 110k, 50k.
- Remove the outliers (1M and 3k).
- Remaining 10 videos: Average = ~100k views.

**Quick Tip:** Sometimes you can eyeball it. If the views are consistent (e.g., 100k, 80k, 120k, 90k), you can estimate around 100k. If the views are wildly inconsistent, rely on your average calculation.

![image.png](https://assets.skool.com/f/866d841bcb40438d86613587f4505c56/a2ed2e65f6d441ec974591d7f30cc067aeb17fc6f183493d917ffed018e66019)

---

##### 2. Get the Influencer’s Price

###### Step-by-Step:

1. Ask the influencer what they charge for the deliverable (e.g., a YouTube video, an Instagram post, etc.).
2. If they’re hesitant to share their price upfront, move to Step 3 to calculate it yourself.

---

##### 3. Calculate the Estimated CPM Price

###### Formula:

**(Average Views / 1,000) x Niche CPM = Estimated Price**

CPM varies by niche, but here are some benchmarks:

**Niche** **CPM ($)** Tech 20-40 Health/Fitness 15-30 Beauty/Lifestyle 10-25 Gaming 10-20 General Entertainment 5-15 Luxury Goods 30-50

###### Example 1: Fair Price

- Influencer charges $3,000 for a YouTube video.
- Average views: 100,000.
- Tech niche CPM: $30.
- **Calculation**: (100,000 / 1,000) x 30 = $3,000.
- Result: Price is fair.

###### Example 2: Overpriced

- Influencer charges $6,000.
- Average views: 100,000.
- Tech niche CPM: $30.
- **Calculation**: (100,000 / 1,000) x 30 = $3,000.
- Result: Price is overpriced. Move to negotiation.

###### Example 3: Underpriced

- Influencer charges $800.
- Average views: 100,000.
- General Entertainment CPM: $10.
- **Calculation**: (100,000 / 1,000) x 10 = $1,000.
- Result: They’re underpricing themselves. You can upsell this influencer to brands for more.

---

##### 4. Factors That Adjust Pricing

###### a. Bonus Factors (Higher Price Justified)

These factors can justify pricing above the calculated CPM:

- **Niche Authority**: Influencer is the top name in their space.
    - Example: MKBHD (a tech YouTuber) charges $20,000 for a video with 500,000 views because he’s the most trusted name in tech.
- **Exclusive Content**: Rarely works with brands, making them more valuable.
- **High-Quality Production**: Professional-level editing and storytelling.
- **Loyal Audience**: Strong trust and engagement (e.g., high comment-to-view ratio).

###### b. Malus Factors (Lower Price Must Be Negotiated)

These factors justify pricing below the calculated CPM:

- **Low Engagement**: Engagement rate under 2% is a red flag.
    - Example: An influencer with 100,000 views gets only 100 likes and 10 comments. Low trust = lower value.
- **Oversaturation**: Too many sponsored posts can fatigue their audience.
- **Inexperience**: Younger or newer influencers often undervalue themselves.
    - Example: A 16-year-old TikToker may accept $500 for a campaign despite having 100,000 views.

---

##### 5. How to Use This Information

###### Step 1: Compare Their Price to Your Calculation

- If the price matches or is slightly higher: Fair, but negotiate down (10-20%).
- If the price is significantly higher: It’s overpriced; negotiate aggressively but politely.
- If the price is lower: Great deal! Don’t negotiate too hard but ensure you leave room for agency profit.

###### Step 2: Negotiate with Tact

Frame your negotiations in a way that’s hard to refuse:

- Example: “Typically, creators in your niche with your views charge closer to $2,500. Could we land around there?”
- Bonus Tip: For brands with tight budgets, suggest switching from a flat rate to CPM-based pricing.

---

##### 6. Develop a Sniff

Pricing isn’t always black and white. Over time, you’ll develop an instinct for:

- **High-quality influencers:** Higher pricing is okay.
- **Low trust or oversaturated influencers:** Push for lower pricing.
- **Young or inexperienced creators:** Easier to negotiate down.

###### Example:

- A 16-year-old gaming YouTuber with 200,000 views per video = Lowball opportunity.
- A 35-year-old fitness coach with a mature, affluent audience = Higher price is justified.

---

##### 7. When to Walk Away

Sometimes, it’s better to move on:

- If the influencer’s price is way off your CPM calculations and they refuse to adjust. Don't burn the bridges, just say "we'll look if anything can fit within that range and get back to you if it does". Keep them in your pocket.
- Example: Influencer quotes $10,000 for content that calculates at $2,000. If they won’t budge, don’t waste time. Again, don't burn the bridges. Keep them in your pocket.

---

##### Final Notes

Pricing is part logic, part intuition.

Use this guide to start, but remember that **experience is the ultimate teacher.**

You’ll refine your approach over time and develop a sniff for what’s fair, overpriced, or a great deal.

Keep learning and keep experimenting, you'll get there bi idni Allah`

##### Introduction: Why Negotiation is the Key

Negotiating isn’t just a step in the process—it’s **the process**. Every dollar you save when working with an influencer translates directly to higher profits for your agency. It’s not about being needy, greedy, or pushy. It’s about being:

- **Strategic**: Knowing how and when to push.
- **Adaptable**: Adjusting your tone to the person you’re speaking with.
- **Confident**: Maintaining control of the conversation and power dynamics.

Why does this matter?

3. **Never leave money on the table** or burn bridges.

- **Be strategic underneath**: You’re firm on the numbers and confident in your approach.

###### 1. The Power Balance

Every negotiation has a power dynamic:

- **Lead the conversation**: If you’re the one steering the questions and framing the offers, you’re in control.
- **Avoid looking needy**: Don’t use language like, *"We really need this to work."* Instead, say something like:
    > *"We love working with influencers like you. Let’s see how we can align on budget and make this happen."*

###### 2. Maintaining Power

How do you keep control?

1. **Ask questions:** Keep the influencer responding to you, not the other way around.
    - Example: *"Would you be against working closer to $3K for this campaign? We've got kind of a budget friendly campaign going here, but would love to have you onboard bigger campaigns in the future!"*

    - *"Totally understand your perspective. For this specific campaign, though, the budget is capped at $4000 per influencer. We're already making a move offering $5k, but to be honest, that would be the max room we have to play with. Let’s explore how we can align here, we can maybe make the deliverables easier to produce on your end?"*
    **Psychological Rule: Always Aim for 30-50% Off**

    Example:
    - Influencer asks for **$8K**
    - Your goal: Bring them down to **$4K-$5K**
    - Key phrase: *“Typically, in this industry, we work within a $4K budget. Can we align there?”*
    Why this works:
    - You sound informed, not desperate.
    - You set an anchor that makes your offer feel reasonable.
    - You frame it around **industry standards**, not your personal preference.

#### Framing Techniques: Make It Hard to Say No

Framing is the heart of negotiation. It’s all about presenting your offers in ways that feel reasonable, collaborative, and hard to refuse.

###### 2. Reverse Framing

- Why it works:
    - No one wants to look inflexible.

Why this works:

- It **removes pressure** from this one deal.
- It makes influencers **more willing to compromise** for future projects.
- It **doesn’t imply the brand is broke**, just that they’re testing ROI first.

> *"If budget is tight, would you be open to a $3K flat rate plus a performance-based bonus or would that be insane?"*

- Why it works:
    - It keeps the conversation open.
    - Shows flexibility while maintaining your budget constraints.

###### 5. Anchoring

> *"We’ve worked with $3K budgets in this niche—if I did a backflip and landed on my feets, any chance we'd align closer to that?"*

- Why it works:
    - Sets expectations early.
    - Frames your offer as reasonable within the market context.

- **YouTube (mid-sized influencers)**: $1,000–$8,000 for integrations; $10,000+ for dedicated videos.
- **Big Influencers (1M+ followers)**: $10,000–$100,000+ for integrations; $50,000+ for dedicated.

**Then, with each niche, it varies : **

- **Health / Productivity**: $1,500–$5,000 for mid-sized influencers.

- **Luxury Goods**: $5,000–$15,000 or more for certain campaigns.

Numbers above are just examples, use the CPM calculation methods to gauge if your influencer is overpriced, fairly priced, or underpriced, then move to step 2.

#### Step 2: Evaluate the Influencer’s Position

1. **Is he Overpriced**?
    - They’re quoting a rate that’s way above your known budget range.
    - Example: Influencer wants $10K, but typical brand spend is $3K–$5K.
2. **Fairly Priced**:
    - Their rate aligns with standard CPM or your known brand budget.
    - Example: Mid-sized influencer says $3K, which fits the typical $2K–$4K range.
3. **Underpriced**:
    - They’re charging significantly less than you know they could.
    - Example: Influencer wants $500, but you can easily sell them to brands at $2,500–$3K.

**Why This Matters**

- **Overpriced**: You’ll need to push for big discounts (40–60%, maybe 70–80%).
- **Fairly Priced**: You want to negotiate to squeeze in your profit. (30–50%)
- **Underpriced**: You can upsell them for a massive margin.

---

#### Step 3: Start Negotiating (Counter-Offer)

Now that you know where your influencer stands (overpriced, fairly priced, or underpriced), it’s time to negotiate.

The key is to stay professional and friendly.

You don’t want to burn bridges—sometimes, you won’t get a deal now, but you might in the future when you can wave real money in front of them.

##### BEFORE YOU START : Learn The General Negotiation Guidelines

1. **Always Stay Polite and Friendly**
    - You’re not going head-to-head in a fight. Keep it calm, respectful, and collaborative. You either win the argument or you win the sale, chose wisely.
2. **Never Burn Bridges**
    - If you can’t reach an agreement, thank them, wish them well, and keep their contact info. You never know when a new brand budget might appear.
3. **No Aggressive Confrontation**
    - Even if their price is outrageous, point it out politely and back it up with logic (“Typically, we see $X in this niche”).
4. **Highlight Future Deals // Long Term Potential**
    - Let them know you have multiple upcoming campaigns or that the brand might come back for more if things go well.
5. **Don’t Sound Desperate (don't be needy, please)**
    - If they sense you’re desperate, they’ll hold firm on their rate. Keep the ball in your court by calmly stating budgets and walking away if needed.

---

###### How to Approach Each Position

1. **Overpriced Influencer**
    - **Tactic**: Hit them with a strong counteroffer that’s significantly lower (40–60% off, sometimes more). Don’t be afraid to let them know you’ll pass if they don’t come closer to your target.
    - **Example**:
          > “I appreciate you sharing your rate of $10K. Realistically, for this type of campaign, we usually land around $4K. Would that be a dealbreaker for you?”
    - If they won’t budge, politely end the convo:
          > “I totally understand. Let’s stay in touch—if we get a brand with a higher budget, I’ll reach out.”
    - **Pro Tip**: Take note of the brands that actually pay them that high; you might pitch those brands yourself later (they got crazy money). Just don’t burn the bridge with the influencer.
2. **Fairly Priced Influencer**
    - **Tactic**: They’re close to the right rate, so you won’t push as hard. Use a gentler approach and see if you can shave off 20–30%. Try 40%-50% if you have high testosterone.
    - **Example**:
          > “$3K sounds fair for your audience. We do have a tighter budget around $2K–$2.4K for this campaign, though. Would you be willing to meet us there?”
    - Don’t insult them by lowballing a decent price too aggressively. Just enough to increase your margin while keeping the relationship intact.
    - If they say no, respect it and leave the door open for next time.
3. **Underpriced Influencer**
    - **Tactic**: Lock them in at (or slightly below) their rate without revealing they’re too cheap.
    - **Example**:
          Needy :
          > “Awesome, I think I can work with $500. If a new opportunity pops up, would it be a dealbreaker if the brand only had $400? I’m just seeing how flexible you are.”
          Aikido master :
          > “I'll try $500 and let you know if anything sticks. Just in case, if a new opportunity pops up, would it be a dealbreaker if we could only pay $400? I’m just seeing how inflexible you are.”
    - They’re happy making $500, so they’ll likely meet you at $400–$450 if they really want the deal. Meanwhile, you can turn around and sell them to the brand at $2K–$3K.
    - Everyone wins: the influencer gets the price they’re already comfortable with, the brand gets a great deal compared to bigger creators, and you make a healthy profit in the middle.

#### 2) PRACTICE (SCENARIOS)

Now that you have the step-by-step theory, let’s see how it applies in real life. Below are common situations you’ll face, along with exactly what to say.

**Situation**

**Action**

    > “I hear you. Let me check with the brand, but in similar campaigns, they’ve spent about $3.5K–$4K. Let’s see if we can align.”

    - If they agree to $4K or something near, great.

###### Scenario 2: The Fairly Priced Influencer (You Still Want More Margin)

          > “Things are tight; can you do $400? Let’s just get this done, and hopefully we’ll collaborate more in the future.”
3. **Upsell to the Brand**
    - Pitch them to brands at $2,500 or $3,000.
    - Everyone’s happy.

---

###### Extra Scenario : The Tough Negotiator (High Ego)

**Situation**

**Action**

    > “Understood. I’ll definitely come back when we have a bigger budget campaign that fits your rate.”
3. **Outcome**
    - You keep the door open for future opportunities.
