### Common Influencer Marketing Terms

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=c301d820a7fd481d984bb8fc50def7ab)*

---

#### Summary

This guide explains key terms like exclusivity, usage, timeline, production date, and placement in influencer marketing. Each term is detailed with examples, helping you understand how to negotiate effectively with brands.

---

##### Influencer Marketing Terms Explained

###### Exclusivity

Exclusivity can mean two things:

1. **Managing an Influencer Exclusively**
    - You act as their exclusive agent.
    - Handle both inbound and outbound deals for them.
    - Makes it easier for brands to trust you since you represent the influencer directly, avoiding potential messes like price disputes or delayed deliverables.
    - Example: You tell a brand,* "I exclusively manage this influencer, so all deals go through me."*
2. **Brand-Specific Exclusivity**
    - Influencers work exclusively with one brand in a niche for a set period.
    - Example:
          - A beard oil brand wants Mr. Beast to advertise for them but doesn’t want him promoting other skincare or beard oil brands during the agreement.
          - This ensures the audience doesn’t lose trust because he isn’t promoting similar products repeatedly.

---

###### Usage

Usage defines how the content created by the influencer can be used by the brand.

- **Basic Usage**: 90-second integration with no strings attached.
- **Expanded Usage**:
    - Restrictions like no competing brand collaborations for a certain time.
    - Full rights to reuse the video for ads.
- Example:
    - Mr. Beast promotes Honey in his video. The brand then pays him for the ad rights to keep running his endorsement as skippable YouTube ads.

---

###### Timeline

Timeline refers to all key dates related to content creation and posting.

- Include specifics like:
    - Contract signing and payment deadline.
    - Product shipping date (if needed).
    - Draft submission date for review.
    - Final posting date.
- Example:
    - January 1: Contract signed and payment received.
    - January 5: Product received for video production.
    - January 15: Draft submitted for approval.
    - January 25: Final video goes live.

---

###### Production Date

- When the video is being created or recorded.
- Make sure all products are shipped in advance so they’re available for filming.
- Example: If the video integrates a physical product, delays in shipping might force influencers to reshoot parts of the video to add the product, which isn’t ideal.

---

###### Placement

Placement refers to where in the video the brand’s ad or mention appears.

- **Options include**:
    - Pre-roll: Mention at the very beginning of the video.
    - Mid-roll: Integrated in the middle after a topic is introduced.
    - Post-roll: Near the end of the video.
- Example:
    - For an iPhone 16 review, the influencer might first discuss the phone to build hype, then transition into discussing the sponsored app.
    - Alternatively, the sponsor could appear right at the beginning before any iPhone details are revealed.

---

##### Summary

- **Exclusivity**: An agreement where either an influencer is exclusively managed by one agent or promotes only one brand in a specific category for a defined period.
- **Usage**: Defines how a brand can repurpose the influencer’s content, including rights for advertising and limiting collaborations with competing brands.
- **Timeline**: A detailed plan outlining key dates for signing contracts, submitting drafts, and publishing content.
- **Production Date**: The date when the content is created, ensuring all necessary products are available beforehand.
- **Placement**: Specifies where in the video the sponsorship will appear, such as at the start, middle, or end.

**These terms help establish clear expectations and smooth collaboration between influencers and brands.**

### Do I need a new Instagram account?

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=a424c109439f4904b27c4a3578257321)*

No, stick with your current account.

### How can I follow up mid-conversation?

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=2f77a082d7c14e51a4195b5cb6149a2f)*

Same rule as with initial follow-ups: don’t be pushy.

Keep it professional, and only do it on **working days**.

If you sent your last message late Friday, don’t follow up first thing Monday—there hasn’t been enough time for a real working period.

Monday is usually hectic for most people anyway.

The goal here is to nudge someone who saw your message but didn’t reply because they forgot.

You want to remind them, not spam them. If there hasn’t been a solid workday window between your last message and the follow-up, **wait it out**.

For example, let’s say you sent the message Friday at 4 PM. Don’t follow up first thing Monday morning.

Wait until **Tuesday afternoon**—that way, they’ve had time to get through the usual Monday madness and can actually pay attention to your message.

Same applies if there’s a holiday or something; don’t follow up on the first possible day.

Always think: "Have they had enough time to realistically respond?" If not, give them a bit more time.

You’re just trying to keep the conversation alive, not annoy them.

PS : That doesn't mean stop after 1 followup, you should keep following up until they respond, while delaying each follow up more and more to avoid looking spammy and robotic.

### When should I use a light or heavy follow-up?

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=8446f635c3f04ba89e2d9c254ed15cd2)*

It’s all about keeping the relationship healthy.

Even if you’re frustrated or need to push harder, don’t kill the vibe.

**Be nice**, use humor, memes, or GIFs, and bring up any issues in a way that keeps the conversation going without making it confrontational.

If you come in too hard, you’ll end up talking to their **ego**, not them, and that’s a fast way to lose the deal.

Use **light follow-ups** most of the time—things like:

"Hey, just bumping this to the top of your inbox in case it got buried. Looking forward to your thoughts!"

These types of follow-ups are great for keeping the convo alive without annoying them.

A funny meme can soften the follow-up and make you memorable.

But, when things are getting serious and you’re not getting the response you need, it’s time for a **heavier follow-up**.

Let’s say the deal is on the line and they’re just not responding:

"Hey [Name], we’ve got the brand ready to launch the campaign next week. But without a response from you, they’re considering canceling the $3K deal and moving on."

With this, you’re still professional, but you’re applying pressure in a respectful way. You make it clear that they’re risking something without turning the conversation into a conflict.

### Followed up 7 times, no responses, what to do ?

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=d995378f446c4ed2afad1ab4ae90e53e)*

**S**o, you've followed up 7 times and still no response?

Don't worry, here's what you can do:

---

**Start a New Email Thread with a Different Approach:**

- **Change the Subject Line:**
    - Make it fresh and interesting.
- **Try a Different Angle:**
    - If you first asked about partnerships, now maybe offer them a specific deal.
    - Or mention that you manage other influencers and have new opportunities.

**Follow Up Again:**

- **Send the New Thread and Follow Up Up to 7 Times:**
    - Keep trying different approaches.
    - Be patient but persistent.

---

**If a Brand is Ghosting You:**

- **Reach Out to Other Employees:**
    - Find someone else at the company to email.
    - Maybe the person you're emailing is busy or not the right person.
    - Ask to be redirected if needed.
    - You can also reach out to them on other platforms (Twitter, Discord, Instagram, Linkedin), you can comment under their posts and ask them to DM you.Then ask for their email or to be redirected and move from there.
- **Ask About Their Current Situation:**
    - See if they're still open to partnerships.
    - Mention that you saw they worked with another influencer (like "I noticed you teamed up with XYZ influencer recently").

**Example:**

"Hi [New Contact Name],

I've been trying to reach out about a possible partnership but haven't heard back. Are you the right person to talk to? I saw you worked with [XYZ Influencer], and I think I have someone who could be a great fit for your next campaign."

*By being specific, you increase your chances of them responding to you ! Don't hesitate to mention influencers name, other brands you've worked with and even competitors !*

---

**If an Influencer is Ghosting You:**

- **Leverage Recent Successes:**
    - Let them know you recently closed a few deals for influencers similar to him.
    - Mention that you are in touch with some brands and some opportunity are right around the corner, you're just waiting for their green flag.
- **Wave Cash Under Their Nose:**
    - Tell them you have a brand ready to work with them. (if it's the case)
    - If not, leverage other things you've been doing in the meantime. (closed new influencers, launching a campaign with XYZ brands, etc).

**Example:**

"Hey [Influencer's Name],

Just wanted to touch base. I recently helped [Another Influencer] land a big deal with [Brand]  and they're looking for more talent to work with. I think I may be able to hook you in, let me know if you're interested."

---

Keep it going this way.

Don't give up!

Sometimes all it takes is a new angle or a little "cash waved under their nose" to get a response.

Remember, if one approach isn't working, switch it up.

Try new things, and keep reaching out.

If an approach worked better than others with some influencers, recycle it and use it again !

### Should I apply for forms on websites?

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=69adf95251cd4be5b344382fa18cbc40)*

No, forget about those forms.

They’re a dead end 99% of the time. Most brands don’t even bother checking them, and even if they do, it’s not going to land you anywhere significant.

That whole “fill out this form and we’ll get back to you” is basically a myth.

Here’s what you should do instead:

1. **LinkedIn is your best friend.**

Go on LinkedIn, type the name of the company you’re targeting, and find the key people. You want to look for job titles like **Marketing Director**, **Influencer Marketing Manager**, or similar.

These are the decision-makers you need to connect with. Don’t waste your time with random employees—they won’t help.

2. **DM them directly.**

Send them a message explaining why you’re reaching out, and **ask to be connected** to the right person if they aren’t the one handling influencer partnerships.

Once you reach that person, keep it short and professional.

Ex : "Hey I'm reaching out on behalf of influencer X because I saw you worked with influencer Y and I was curious if you'd be open to working with more influencers, can't wait to speak about you"

LinkedIn works way better than those anonymous website forms because you’re cutting straight to the real people who make the decisions.

**3. Find their email.**

LinkedIn is great, but **don’t rely on it alone**. Sending an email alongside your LinkedIn message will increase your chances of getting a response. Here’s how to find the email:

Let’s say you found the **Marketing Director** on LinkedIn. His name is **Mahmoud** and he works for **Amazon**. Try common email formats like [**Mahmoud@amazon.com**](mailto:Mahmoud@amazon.com) or [**Mahmoud.lastname@amazon.com**](mailto:Mahmoud.lastname@amazon.com). These formats work most of the time, especially with larger companies.

If you don’t get a hit with that, go to the company’s website. Head to the **Privacy Policy** or **Terms & Conditions** page. Hit **Ctrl+F** and search for the “@” symbol. Brands often have emails hidden there—usually for customer service or legal stuff, but that’s still a foot in the door. Once you’ve got an email, introduce yourself and ask to be directed to the right person if needed.

**4. Don’t ignore general emails.**

If you can’t find the specific contact’s email or want to cover all your bases, it’s totally fine to send your message to the company’s general email (like [**info@company.com**](mailto:info@company.com) or [**support@company.com**](mailto:support@company.com)). Just make sure to ask to be redirected to the right person. Something like, “Hi, I’m trying to reach the team handling influencer collaborations. Could you point me in the right direction?”

Even if you don’t get to the exact person right away, this can still get you a response and redirect you to the right contact.

**Bottom line:** Don’t waste time on website forms. Use LinkedIn to find the key people, and back it up with an email. That combo gives you a much better chance of getting a response and getting the ball rolling.

### How to Get a Brand's Budget?

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=1f25ab4e40ef44a4b6a420d606915a7b)*

---

#### Summary :

To get a brand's budget, your goal is to ask it without them **noticing**.

We'll mainly try and get the max they can spend, and what they're looking for here.

You can do so by :

1. **Asking budget-related questions upfront**:
    > **Example questions to ask**:
    > - “Should we include influencers above or below $20K, or is that out of your budget?”
    > - “We’ve got creators with 1M+ subscribers and 500K average views. What do you usually pay for this type of creator?”

---

#### Detailed answer

##### Introduction

If you're trying to get a brand's budget estimate to make a strong offer—or struggling to get responses after sending influencer lists—this guide breaks down effective strategies.

The key is **pre-handling objections** before discussing the price.

This isn't just a business tip; it's a proven strategy that sets the right tone and increases your success rate.

---

##### Why Pre-Handling Objections Matters

When you pre-handle objections:

- It positions your communication as logistical and helpful, not salesy.
- It reduces resistance and keeps the focus on decision-making rather than negotiation.
- Brands perceive you as aligned with their goals, not just after their budget.

On the flip side, dropping a price before handling objections can backfire.

Brands may dismiss your offer as disingenuous, regardless of how well you justify it later.

---

##### The Right Approach: Pre-Objection Handling

###### General Strategy

When pitching your influencers, **don’t immediately send a list** of names and prices.

Instead, follow these steps:

1. **Start small**:
    - Use the *foot-in-the-door method*. Begin with one influencer—the best option for their niche and goals—before sending a complete list.
    - This reduces overwhelm and eliminates fears of large upfront costs.
2. **Ask budget-related questions upfront**:
    - Phrase these questions as logistical help to make the process easier for the brand.
    - Avoid coming across as greedy or focused solely on extracting their full budget.
3. **Example questions to ask**:
    - “Should we include influencers above or below $20K, or is that out of your budget?”
    - “We’ve got creators with 1M+ subscribers and 500K average views. What do you usually pay for this type of creator?”

These questions:

- Help brands frame their expectations.
- Prevent pricing objections later since they’ve already defined their range.
- Allow you to tailor your offer to their budget.

---

##### Practical Application: Email Structure

Here’s how to position yourself as a helpful partner:

###### What to Say

- *“We’ve shortlisted a few influencers for your project. To save time and avoid sending names outside your budget, should we focus on those above or below $20K?”*
- *“We have influencers at various price points depending on their niche and performance metrics. Could you share what you usually pay for creators with similar reach and engagement?”*

###### What NOT to Say

- Avoid: *“What’s your budget so I can adjust the price?”*
    - This feels transactional and self-serving.

Instead:

- Frame your inquiry as a way to help them:
    - *“We want to avoid wasting your time with influencers outside your range.”*
    - *“Let us handpick options that best suit your campaign and budget.”*

---

##### The Psychology of Sending a List

**Why Brands Ignore Big Lists**:

- A large list with high prices (e.g., $50K total) can overwhelm smaller brands.
- They may assume:
    - Your agency only works with big companies.
    - The influencers are overpriced.
    - The collaboration doesn’t fit their scale.

**Solution**: Start small with one influencer and use it as an entry point.

---

##### Handling Objections: Key Tips

1. **Understand their situation first**:
    - Ask questions that clarify their goals, preferences, and past collaborations.
    - Example: *“Are you more focused on ROI-driven investments or aiming for top-tier creators?”*
2. **Leverage their responses**:
    - Build your pitch around the answers they provide.
    - If they name a budget, align your offer within that range.
3. **Prevent Price Objections**:
    - By getting budget clarity upfront, any issues will be about value—not affordability.

---

##### Aikido Questions for Better Clarity

Use these “soft” questions to guide the conversation:

- *“Would you prefer ROI-focused creators or top-tier ones?”*
- *“Some influencers are highly competitive; others are premium-priced. Which type suits your campaign better?”*
- *“Are you looking to test smaller creators or make a bigger impact with established names?”*

These questions:

- Show you’re thinking from their perspective.
- Help you gather actionable insights for tailoring your offer.

---

##### Advanced Tips

1. **Segment Your Lists**:
    - If you’re working with a large number of influencers, let brands know you’ll handpick the best fits based on their budget and goals.
    - Example: *“We’ll segment and share influencers most relevant to your campaign to save you time and effort.”*
2. **Avoid Overloading Small Brands**:
    - If you have 5-10 influencers, don’t mention a “large list.” Reserve that language for lists with 50+ influencers.
3. **Emphasise Convenience**:
    - Highlight how your process saves them hassle:
          - *“Instead of sending a massive list, we’ll focus on curated options aligned with your needs.”*

---

##### Summary

- **Pre-handle objections** by asking thoughtful, budget-focused questions before sending any list or price.
- Avoid overwhelming brands with large lists or high upfront costs.
- Use soft, discovery-based questions to gather insights and build trust.
- Tailor your offers to their preferences, eliminating most objections upfront.

With these strategies, you’ll avoid common pitfalls and make your pitches far more effective.

### Masterclass on calls

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=83094599982f42788a954e1c3c0315b2)*

---

#### Summary

- Calls with clients are not always necessary in marketing.
- If you need to set up a call, make sure it's short and to the point, and prepare in advance to avoid wasting anyone’s time.
- Stay calm and professional, and understand that clients are just people, no need to stress.

---

##### When Should You Set Up Calls?

1. **Big Companies (e.g., NordVPN):**
    - **No Call Needed.**
    Big brands like NordVPN generally don’t do calls. They’re too busy, and your small campaign isn’t worth their time. Don’t expect a call with these companies.
2. **Mid-Sized Companies:**
    - **Do Calls After 1-2 Campaigns.**
    After you’ve run a campaign or two, it can be helpful to schedule a call to build a better relationship. You can say something like:
          - “Hey, the past few campaigns have been great! We’ve been working on some new things and thought a quick chat would be useful. How about Monday?”
          - This is optional, but it helps to show you care and build a relationship.
3. **Small Companies:**
    - **Calls Are Useful.**
    Smaller companies might be more nervous about spending money and might need a call to feel comfortable.
    - Calls with these clients can make them feel more confident in you. They’re often more trusting after a conversation, and it’s easier to close deals once they see you’re legit.
          - ***Tip:**** This is the easiest way to build trust with a smaller company.*

---

##### How to Run the Call

1. **Prepare Before the Call:**
    - **Spend 15 Minutes Getting Ready.**
    Don’t just jump into the call. Take a moment to review what you want to ask and research the company. This preparation will make you look 10 times better and help you avoid wasting time.
2. **Call Duration:**
    - **Keep it Short.**
    Aim for a **30-minute** call. If the conversation goes longer, that’s fine, but don’t plan for an hour unless it’s needed. Clients usually don’t want long calls.
          - If you need to go over campaign details, 30 minutes is enough.
3. **First 3-5 Minutes:**
    - **Start Casual.**
    The first 3 minutes are crucial. Don’t dive straight into business—be human. You can say something like:
          - “Hey, how’s everything going today? Where are you calling from? Oh, I see you have a bear in the background, haha!”
          - Crack a joke, but don’t overdo it. Keep it light and friendly.
          - Then, **explain the call agenda**: “We’ve got 30 minutes to chat about the campaign. I’d love to hear your thoughts on the main things you want to focus on to make sure we exceed expectations.”
4. **Structure the Call:**
    - **Ask the Right Questions.**
    Once the small talk is done, move to the key questions:
          - “What have you done with influencers before?”
          - “What are the main things you’re expecting from this campaign?”
    - **Clarify Expectations:**
    Set realistic expectations so you can exceed them. Make sure they understand your goals.

---

##### Attitude During the Call

- **Don’t Overthink It.**
  You don’t need to be stressed or try to be perfect. Take calls from wherever you feel comfortable—whether in your car, at a desk, or outside.
    - **What to Wear:** Don’t stress about your outfit. Wear what you’re comfortable in—a T-shirt is fine. Just look professional.
    - **Use Headphones or a Mic:** Don’t use big gaming headsets. If you have AirPods, use them, if not just use your speakers.
    - **Stay Calm:** Clients are regular people. They’re normal humans just like you. Whether they’re worth millions or nothing, they’re just humans. **If you’re nervous, they’ll notice, and it will hurt your chances.** So, find a way to relax before the call.

---

##### Conclusion

Calls aren’t always necessary, but when they are, **keep them short and focused**. Be prepared, stay calm, and don’t stress about being perfect. Remember, clients are human too, and they’ll trust you more if you act confident and relaxed.

### How to find qualified brands

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=f1e0e81f42a44edea039a4f01d991a56)*

---

#### Summary

To find 300+ qualified brands, check the top 10 influencers in your niche, go through their last 50 pieces of content, and identify sponsors. Repeat this for 30 influencers, and you'll find a lot of brands.

---

##### How to Find 300+ Qualified Brands

###### Step 1: Find Top Influencers in Your Niche

- Look at the **top 10 influencers** in your niche.
- Focus on influencers who have **5 million followers or more**.

###### Step 2: Analyze Their Content

- Go through **the last 50 pieces of content** from each influencer.

    - Streamers tend to have **very engaged audiences** who tune in regularly and interact with them in real-time. This means that when a streamer recommends a product or brand, it feels more authentic to their viewers. You’re getting **real-time reactions** and interactions, which is a big advantage over traditional influencer content.

    - Streamers usually work with brands on a **longer-term basis**. Instead of one-off posts, they mention the brand over multiple streams, sometimes for weeks or months. This gives the brand **consistent visibility** rather than just a single moment of attention.

    - You have more ways to integrate the brand. A streamer can do **live product reviews**, **unboxings**, or even **giveaways** during their stream. This variety creates more dynamic content compared to static posts or stories.

### Turn a 'No' into 'Yes' with This Simple Technique

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=b3e5ecd684c8406fa706eee6ee588829)*

#### Summary

When you face rejection from a company or influencer, acknowledge their response without being pushy or defensive.

Then, use a clever two-option question to uncover their true reason and guide the conversation back to your advantage.

---

##### Step-by-Step Guide

###### Step 1: Acknowledge the Rejection

- Don’t react emotionally.
- Avoid saying things like:
    - “Oh, why?”
    - “Oh, you’re wrong!”
    - “But you made a mistake!”
- Instead, stay calm and understanding:
    - *“Cool, no problem. Totally understand. No worries.”*

---

###### Step 2: Avoid Sounding Needy

- Don’t beg or push for answers. It kills the conversation.
- Refrain from asking open-ended questions like, *“How can I fix it?”* because it sounds desperate.

---

###### Step 3: Use the "Two-Choices Technique"

- Offer them two options where one is obviously wrong.
- This makes them reveal their real reason without feeling pressured.

###### Example 1: Brand Rejecting an Influencer

- Scenario: A brand says, *“At the moment, we’re not interested.”*
- Your response:
    - *“Cool, no problem. Totally understand. I was just curious—are you completely against influencer marketing, or was it just this specific creator that wasn’t the right fit?”*
    - **Why this works:**
          - You already know they do influencer marketing.
          - They’ll likely admit the real issue (e.g., wrong creator).
          - Now you can suggest a better fit or prove the creator’s value.

###### Example 2: Influencer Rejecting a Deal

- Scenario: An influencer says, *“I’m not interested.”*
- Your response:
    - *“No problem at all, just so I make sure I understand—are you planning to stop doing partnerships altogether, or is it just the price that doesn’t work for you?”*
    - **Why this works:**
          - If they’re still open to partnerships, you can address their concerns (e.g., pricing).

---

###### Why This Technique Works

- You’re subtly leading the conversation.
- The wrong option makes them choose the real issue.
- It avoids them feeling attacked or pressured.
- You get useful information to adjust your offer.

---

###### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Don’t argue or debate their decision.**
    - Wrong: *“Oh, but you made a mistake!”*
    - Right: *“Cool, no problem. Totally understand.”*
- **Don’t sound needy.**
    - Wrong: *“How can I fix this? Please reconsider!”*
    - Right: Use two-options to uncover their issue.

---

###### Conclusion

Getting a "no" isn’t the end. Acknowledge it, stay calm, and use the two-choice technique to guide the conversation back on track. It’s a smooth, confident way to handle objections while learning what’s really holding them back.

---

##### Summary

1. Stay calm and acknowledge the rejection.
2. Avoid sounding desperate or defensive.
3. Use a two-choice question to uncover the real issue.
4. Adjust your approach based on their response.

**This method keeps the conversation productive and increases your chances of turning a “no” into a “yes.”**

### Effective Objection Handling in Sales

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=f1fc240a8dbf43a69b0ddb909236c937)*

---

#### Summary

The key to handling objections in sales is isolating the objection. Find out the real problem preventing the deal and make sure it’s the only issue before addressing it. This prevents wasting time or resources on things that aren’t the real problem.

---

###### Step 1: Understand the Importance of Isolating Objections

- Before addressing any objection, ensure that it’s the only reason blocking the deal.
- Don’t waste time or money fixing issues that may not be the actual problem.

**Example:**
If an influencer refuses to work with your company, don’t immediately assume it’s about the price. It could be the company, the offer, or even the timeline. Addressing price first without knowing the root cause could lead to unnecessary losses.

---

###### Step 2: How to Isolate Objections

1. **Ask Specific Questions:**
    - Your goal is to clarify objections and ensure influencers or brands feel confident in your offer. Use targeted questions to uncover the real problem while confirming other aspects of the deal.
          Examples:
          - *"Budget aside, does everything else about the collaboration work for you?"*
          - *"Do you like the brand's mission and values?"*
          - *"Do you trust us to deliver the kind of partnership you're looking for?"*
          These questions help break down doubts about key elements like compatibility with the brand, trust in your agency, or the overall structure of the deal.
2. **Identify the Core Problem:**
    - Walk step by step through potential concerns to pinpoint the real issue. This avoids unnecessary adjustments that might not address the root objection.
          Examples:
          - If an influencer says, *"I'm not sure I want to work with this brand,"* dig deeper:
                  - *"Is it the product? The brand's image? The timeline? Or something else?"*
          - If the problem is budget-related, you can ask:
                  - *"If we can adjust the payment terms or find another solution, would you feel comfortable moving forward?"*
          The goal is to identify the exact barrier so you can address it directly.
3. **Cover All Angles:**
    - Ensure no other hidden objections remain.
    - Example:
          - If someone hesitates because you lack case studies or a website, confirm if fixing one issue solves the problem or if there’s something else.

---

###### Step 3: Adjust for Context (B2C vs. B2B)

- **For Customers (B2C):**
    - Customers often make emotional decisions.
    - Dive deep into objections and connect on a personal level.
- **For Business Clients (B2B):**
    - Logical decisions dominate.
    - Focus on providing clear, logical solutions that address specific concerns.

---

###### Real-Life Example for Sales Calls

- When selling your product or service:
    - If someone says, *"I need to think about it,"* don’t leave the conversation vague.
    - Respond:
          - *"No problem, I understand. What exactly do you need to think about? The product, me, or the price?"*
          - *"Let’s figure it out together since I built the product and can help clarify any doubts."*
    - Break it down until only one objection remains, then handle it.

---

###### Example for Brands and Influencers

- **For Influencers:**
    - If an influencer doesn’t reply, figure out why:
          - Is it the lack of a website?
          - Is it your budget?
          - Is it the partnership itself?
    - Confirm each aspect and address the exact issue.
- **For Brands:**
    - If a brand hesitates, ask:
          - *"Is it the influencer?"*
          - *"Is it our past case studies?"*
          - *"Is it the timeline?"*

---

##### Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Addressing the Wrong Objection:**
    - Fixing price when the issue is trust or a missing case study.
2. **Not Isolating All Objections:**
    - If one objection remains hidden, it may surface later and ruin the deal.
3. **Rushing the Process:**
    - Take time to confirm all aspects before moving forward.

---

##### Conclusion

Objection handling is all about isolation. Identify the real issue, confirm there’s nothing else blocking the deal, and address the core concern directly.

Whether it’s about price, trust, or missing elements, isolating the objection ensures a smooth and successful close.

### How to Write Good Emails

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=627ff44fb3e04d289328180cfa81ec95)*

---

#### Summary

To write better emails, ensure they have a *simple flow*. Stick to one clear idea, connect all points logically, and end with a clear call-to-action (CTA).

Avoid stacking random thoughts or leaving the reader confused about what to do next. Make the email impossible to misunderstand and keep the reader engaged throughout.

---

##### How to Write Better Emails: Make Them Flow

A flowing email connects all its parts logically, with each sentence naturally leading into the next. Here’s how to achieve that:

- **One Idea Per Email:** Focus on a single subject throughout the email. Don’t mix unrelated topics.
- **Clear Subject Line:** Your subject line should introduce the purpose of the email. For example:
    - *Partnership Request*
    - *Collaboration Opportunity with Tom*
- **Logical Connections:** Connect each sentence to the next, so the reader easily follows your thought process.

---

###### What Happens When an Email Doesn’t Flow?

Imagine reading an email that’s all over the place. For example:

**“Hey, I hope you’re doing great. I saw your video. I liked it. Let me know what you think.”**
This doesn’t connect well. The reader is left wondering:

- *What do you mean by "what I think"? Think about what?*
- *What do you actually want from me?*

The CTA (*"Let me know what you think"*) is vague and doesn’t give the reader clear direction.

---

###### What Does a Flowing Email Look Like?

Here’s an example of a good email flow:

- **Subject Line:** Partnership Request
- **Introduction:**
  "Hey [Name], I hope you’re doing amazing. On my end, I’m doing super well because I just watched your last video about burning 50 Lamborghinis—loved it!"
- **Connect the Dots:**
  "I also noticed you worked with NordVPN. Great choice! On the other hand, I saw your video eating 50 burgers didn’t have a sponsor."
- **Clear CTA:**
  "So, I was curious if you’re open to partnerships. Let me know if that’s something you’d like to explore!"

###### Why Does This Work?

- Each part flows into the next.
- The writer connects their observations with a clear reason for reaching out.
- The CTA (call-to-action) is obvious: *Are you open to partnerships?*

---

###### Avoiding Common Mistakes

Here are some mistakes to avoid when crafting emails:

- **Random Thoughts:** Don’t stack unrelated points. For example:
  *"Hey, I hope you’re doing great. I saw your video. It was good. Are you open to partnerships?"*
  While the CTA (*"Are you open to partnerships?"*) is clear, the email feels disconnected because the thoughts leading up to it are not logically tied together. It lacks a smooth flow and context to make the request feel natural.
- **Lack of Context:** Your email shouldn’t leave the reader wondering why you’re reaching out. Build a clear narrative leading to your CTA.

---

###### Key Tips for Better Emails

1. **Read Your Email Aloud:** Check if the flow makes sense.
2. **Think Like the Reader:** Imagine the lowest attention span reader trying to understand. Don’t leave room for confusion.
3. **Connect Sentences:** Link the last sentence to the next.
    - Example:
          - "I saw your video."
          - "Because of that, I wanted to ask..."
4. **CTA Matters:** Make the next step clear and obvious.

---

###### How People Read Emails

Most readers skim. Here’s what they typically do:

1. Read the subject line.
2. Skim the first few sentences.
3. Jump to the CTA.
  If the email is clear, they might read the rest. If not, they’ll skip entirely.

---

###### Do This for Better Results

- **Subject Line:** Clearly state the email’s purpose.
- **Flow:** Connect every thought so the email is smooth and engaging.
- **CTA:** Be specific about what you want the reader to do.
- **No Leaks:** Don’t let their attention "leak out" by being vague or disorganized.

---

###### Summary

To write better emails, craft them like a smooth gradient—each sentence leads naturally to the next. Make your purpose clear in the subject line, connect all points logically, and end with a strong CTA.** This approach boosts your response and closing rates.**
