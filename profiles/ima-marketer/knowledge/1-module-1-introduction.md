### 5. Setting expectations: what you need to know

Setting solid foundations is key to start your business. Here I'm breaking down the basics :

- **4 Hours Per Day**: If you want good results, plan to work **4 hours every day**. This is the sweet spot. It’s enough time to make real progress if you stay focused and consistent.

#### Step-by-Step Guide

**1. Get Educated on Business**

- **Understand Business Basics:** Learn how businesses operate and make money.
- **Learn to Speak with Confidence:** Practice speaking without sounding needy.

- **Work Hours:** Know that you'll need to put in consistent hours of work.

- **Choose a Niche You Understand:** Pick an area you're interested in and know about.

**4. Make a List of Influencers (40% Completed)**

- **List 50-100 Influencers:** Find 50-100 influencers in your chosen niche.

**5. Reach Out to Influencers**

- **Contact Them:** Email these influencers asking if they are open to partnerships.
- **Your Goal:** Get them to reply and to send over their rates and audience informations. Your next step would be to negotiate them down if they have an agent, or to offer to help them get more partnerships if they are not managed already.

    - **Audience Analytics:** Ask for their media kit or details about their audience.
    - **Pricing:** Find out how much they charge.

**6. Negotiate and Close Influencers (50% Completed)**

- **Decide Your Approach:**
    - **Negotiating them down :** Negotiate for them to lower their prices so you can pocket the difference when selling to brands.

- **Make a List of Brands:**
    - **Research Sponsors:** Look at other influencers in your niche and see who sponsors them. (look at the top 10 influencers in your niche + the list of influencers you gathered in the beginning)
- **Find the Right Contacts:**
    - **Email or LinkedIn:** Use email or LinkedIn to find the right person at the brand to contact.
- **Reach Out on Behalf of Your Influencer:**
    - **Personalized Email:** Send a personalized email introducing your influencer.
    - **Ask Questions:**
          - Are they open to working with more influencers?
          - What are they looking for in partnerships?
    - **Offer Your Influencer:** Present your influencer as a great match for their needs.

- **Negotiate Terms:** Work out the details between the brand and your influencer.

### 9. Community Call Etiquettes 📞

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/b5aec20e?md=b9dc537e0ac445b891393a38c82371c4)*

As you know, we have multiple weekly calls within the community (you can find them in the [Calendar](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/calendar) tab). This is one of the most valuable parts of your experience here. To make sure everyone walks away with maximum value, we hold these calls to a high standard. Here's what that looks like.

---

**The Mindset**

These calls are a privilege and like most privileges, they're easy to take for granted until they're gone. Come prepared, stay engaged, and take notes.

You're in the right place, show up for it!

---

**Before the Call**

- Join on time. If you're running late, mute yourself immediately and ease in quietly.
- Find a quiet space with a stable connection. Noise and dropouts pull focus away.
- Have your question ready before you join. Brain dump everything on your mind, then ask Claude to condense it into one clear sentence. Come in with that sentence ready.
    ---

**During the Call**

- Camera on, always. You will be reminded if yours is off.
- Keep your mic muted unless you have the floor.
- Don't start random conversations in the chat. It distracts the coach and other students. Save those for Discord, the group chat, or the Skool community.
- You'll be prompted for reactions using thumbs up 👍, thumbs down 👎, and other emojis. Use those when asked.
    ---

**How to Submit For Calls**

If you want to submit for Roast My Email, or Review My Negotiations, you can find how [HERE](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/718eaac2?md=42be5456768248289583acdd6a56c082).

---

**Asking Questions**

Think beyond the obvious — former colleagues, university friends, gym buddies, LinkedIn connections.

- **Investing and finance** — crypto, real estate, personal finance, entrepreneurship
- **Entertainment and hobbies** — gaming, beauty, food, travel, fashion, sports

**3. How do I know if my niche is actually good?**

Stress test it against these checkpoints. Hit at least 3 of 4:

1. **I can name at least 10 brands actively spending in this niche** — check Instagram sponsored posts or search "which brands spend on influencer marketing in [niche]"
2. **I can find at least 20 relevant creators in under one hour** — search hashtags, keywords, or YouTube categories
3. **Google Trends shows this niche is growing over the past 3–5 years** — type the niche keyword, set to 5-year view, check if the line is trending up
4. **At least one credible source confirms brand spend in this category is growing** — a quick search of "[niche] influencer marketing spend 2025" on Statista or Influencer Marketing Hub is enough

If you can't tick at least 3 within two hours, pick a different niche.

#### Step 5: Set Up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC (To Stay Out of Spam)

These are **authentication records** that prove you’re a real sender — not a scammer or spammer. **This part is CRITICAL.**

###### 1. Add SPF Record

SPF tells email providers **which servers are allowed to send email from your domain.**

Here’s how to add it:

- Go to your DNS settings again
- Add a **TXT record**

- **Host/Name**: `@`
- **Type**: TXT
- **Value**:
    > v=spf1 include:_[spf.google.com](http://spf.google.com) ~all
- Click Save

That’s your SPF set up

---

###### 2. Add DKIM Record

DKIM adds a **digital signature** to your email, which helps Gmail trust you more.

Here’s how to set it up:

- Go to your Google Admin Console: admin.google.com
- Log in using your new email (yourname@yourwebsite.com)
- In the search bar at the top, type:
  **“DKIM”**
- Click: **Authenticate email**
- Choose your domain
- Click: **Generate New Record**
- Copy the **TXT record name and value** Google gives you
- Go back to your DNS settings
- Add a new **TXT record**:
    - **Host/Name**: Paste the DKIM **record name**
    - **Type**: TXT
    - **Value**: Paste the **record value**
- Click Save
- Go back to the Admin Console and click **Start Authentication**

Done

---

###### 3. Add DMARC Record

DMARC tells email providers how to handle emails that **fail SPF or DKIM.** It helps protect your domain from being spoofed.

Here’s a simple setup to start:

- Go back to your DNS
- Add a new **TXT record**:

- **Host/Name**:
    > _dmarc
- **Type**: TXT
- **Value**:
    > v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=[mailto:your@email.com](mailto:your@email.com)
- (Replace `your@email.com` with your real email)

Click Save

This setup is enough to protect your domain and improve email delivery.

*video 18m02s · Skool-hosted video · [open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/b5aec20e?md=4a404d4ed1dc4d54bed723ec4cfc7579) · transcript: `transcripts/m1-30-7-the-most-important-skill-entrepreneurs-need.txt`*

##### Introduction: Why Problem Solving is Everything

Assalamu alaikum wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuh.

Let’s get straight to it: If you want to make money, start a business, or succeed in any entrepreneurial field, you need one foundational skill **above all others**

#### Problem solving

Knowledge? Sure, it’s important.

Self-discipline? Absolutely.

Belief? Can’t do without it.

But **problem-solving** is the *foundation* that holds everything together.

Without it, you can’t even use the other skills effectively.

In this course, I’m going to explain why problem-solving is the single most important skill you need to succeed, how to develop it, and how it impacts everything else.

By the end of this, inshaAllah, you’ll not only understand its importance but also have actionable steps to start improving your problem solving ability today.

---

##### Part 1: The Layers of Success (And Why Problem Solving is on Top)

Imagine your success as a layered pyramid. Each layer builds on the one below it, but the foundation is what makes it all stand firm. Let’s break it down:

1. **Knowledge**:
  This is what most people think they need first—"If I just know how to start a business, I’ll succeed." Knowledge is crucial, but it’s not enough on its own. You can know how to do something, but if you’re not solving problems or applying what you learn, nothing happens.
2. **Self-Discipline**:
  Knowing something isn’t enough; you need the discipline to act on it. Without self-discipline, knowledge just sits there unused. You can’t build success if you’re not willing to put in the work consistently.
3. **Belief**:
  Beneath discipline is belief. If you don’t believe in yourself or the process, you won’t take the actions needed to succeed. You’ll sabotage yourself before you even start.
4. **Problem-Solving Ability**:
  This is the foundation. Without it, none of the other layers can stand. Problem-solving is what allows you to:
    - Figure things out when you’re stuck.
    - Adapt when things don’t go as planned.
    - Build solutions from scratch when there’s no guide or roadmap.
    Think of it this way: If you give someone knowledge but no problem-solving ability, they’ll fail as soon as they hit a roadblock.
    But if you give someone strong problem-solving skills, they can figure out the knowledge they need, overcome obstacles, and find a way forward—no matter what.

---

##### Part 2: Why Problem Solving Beats Everything Else

Let me give you an example to make this clear. Imagine two entrepreneurs:

- **Entrepreneur A** has tons of knowledge about their industry but lacks problem-solving skills. They know what to do in theory, but as soon as they face a challenge they’ve never seen before, they freeze. They rely on others to fix things for them and can’t adapt to unexpected problems.
- **Entrepreneur B** has basic knowledge but is a great problem solver. They don’t know everything, but they’re resourceful. When they encounter a problem, they figure it out. They research, experiment, and keep trying until they find a solution.

Who do you think will succeed in the long run?
**Entrepreneur B.**

---

##### Part 3: How to Develop Problem-Solving Skills

Here’s the good news: Problem-solving is a skill, and like any skill, it can be developed. It takes effort and intentional practice, but it’s absolutely possible. Here’s how:

---

###### 1. Allow Yourself to Fail

- **Understand This: Failure = Learning.**
    - Most people are terrified of failure because they see it as the end. It’s not. Every failure is an opportunity to learn and grow.
    - For example, if you try a strategy and it doesn’t work, you now know what *doesn’t* work. That’s valuable information you didn’t have before.
- **Reframe Failure as Feedback.**
    - Instead of thinking, "I failed," think, "I learned what doesn’t work."
    - This mindset allows you to take risks, try new things, and grow faster than someone who avoids failure at all costs.

---

###### 2. Train Yourself to Think

Most of us don’t actually sit down and think when we encounter problems. Instead, we panic, procrastinate, or immediately ask someone else for help. To become a good problem solver, you need to *train your brain* to think critically.

- **Start by Asking Questions:**
    - What’s the actual problem?
    - Why is this happening?
    - What are potential solutions?
    - Has anyone else solved this problem before? If so, how?
- **Use a Whiteboard or Paper:**
    - Visualizing your thoughts can help you see connections and patterns you might otherwise miss.
    - Write down everything you know about the problem, then brainstorm potential solutions.

---

###### 3. Leverage Resources (But Don’t Depend on Them)

Tools like Google, YouTube, and AI (like ChatGPT) are incredible for solving problems—but only if you use them the right way.

- **Don’t Ask for the Full Answer.**
    - Instead of saying, "Write this email for me," ask: "What are the key components of a good email in this context?"
    - Then take that information and create the email yourself. This way, you’re learning and improving, not just copying.
- **Research Before Asking for Help.**
    - Before reaching out to someone for advice, try to solve the problem on your own first. Even if you don’t fully solve it, the effort will teach you something—and when you do ask for help, you’ll be able to ask better questions.

---

##### Part 4: The Long-Term Benefits of Problem Solving

Problem-solving doesn’t just help you in business—it improves every aspect of your life.

- **In Business:**
    - You’ll adapt to challenges faster.
    - You’ll become more independent and resourceful.
    - You’ll gain confidence in your ability to handle anything that comes your way.
- **In Life:**
    - You’ll approach personal challenges with a calm, solution-focused mindset.
    - You’ll inspire others by showing that problems can be overcome with effort and persistence.

---

##### Final Thought: Why This Skill is So Important

Let me end with one last example to drive this home. Imagine you’re building a house. You have the best materials in the world—top-notch wood, steel, concrete, and so on. But if you build that house on sand, it’s going to collapse.

Your problem-solving ability is the foundation. Without it, everything else—knowledge, discipline, belief—will fall apart. But if you have a strong foundation, you can build anything on top of it and make it work.

So, focus on becoming a great problem solver. Everything else will follow, inshaAllah.

---

##### Quick Recap: How to Start Today

1. **Reframe Failure**: See every problem as an opportunity to learn.
2. **Train Your Thinking**: Spend time analyzing problems and brainstorming solutions before asking for help.
3. **Leverage Resources Wisely**: Use tools to guide your thinking, not to replace it.

With these steps, you’re not just building a business—you’re building yourself into the kind of person who can succeed in any situation.

And that’s the real goal.

See you in the next course, inshaAllah.

### 9. AI Masterclass: How to Use ChatGPT Like a Pro

*[open lesson](https://www.skool.com/the-ima-accelerator-9388/classroom/b5aec20e?md=60576ed016e4437a8aee15fd57b434ca)*

*(AI as a tool for thinking, not replacing)*

#### Disclaimer ⚠️:

All prompts and outputs in this tutorial are purely examples to demonstrate how AI works and how to structure instructions effectively.
Do not reuse these examples in your influencer marketing work or professional communication.
They are illustrations, not ready-made solutions.
You must develop your own prompts and systems tailored to your specific goals and contexts.
If everyone uses identical prompts, creativity and originality vanish.
This guide exists to help you think independently, not to automate thought.

---

#### Core Principle: What Is Prompting?

A prompt is your instruction set, the bridge between your intent and the AI’s output.
It’s how you tell the model what to do, how to do it, and for whom.

AI doesn’t “think” or “understand” the way humans do, it predicts text based on patterns in the data it was trained on.
That means your words act as coordinates. The clearer your map, the closer the AI gets to your intended destination.

Think of prompting as giving GPS directions:

If you say:
“Take me somewhere nice”

You might end up anywhere.

But if you say:
“Take me to the nearest coffee shop with Wi-Fi”

You’ll get a specific, useful result.

---

#### Key Ideas to Remember

• AI is a pattern predictor, not a mind reader. It guesses what’s likely to come next, based on how you frame the request.
• Specificity equals control. Vague prompts let the AI guess your goal, and detailed prompts make it align with your intent.
• Context guides tone and depth. Adding details like audience, purpose, and format helps the AI choose the right “voice.”
• Structure matters. Organizing your instructions with bullets, steps, or roles improves clarity.

**Example**

Bad prompt:
“Explain marketing”

Good prompt:
“As a senior marketing manager, explain the basics of marketing to a first-year business student using short examples.”

See the difference?

The second one gives role, audience, and tone, so the AI can adjust its style and depth.

---

#### Rule: Garbage In → Garbage Out

AI mirrors your clarity.

If your input is vague, biased, or incomplete, your output will reflect that.

Treat every prompt like you’re briefing a new team member. Give it the background, the goal, and the constraints.

---

#### Pro Tip: Aim for “Complete Minimalism”

Give enough details for accuracy, but not so many that you confuse the model.

Your goal is a prompt that’s clear, concise, and actionable.

**Example:**
“Summarize this text in one paragraph, focusing only on the key challenges and solutions, written in plain English.”

That’s short, but complete, and that’s the essence of strong prompting.

---

#### Prompt Layers (Structure of Every Good Prompt)

Each layer adds control. Missing one weakens output precision.
(Ranked from most important to least)

**1. Task – What to Do**
Defines the action.
Example: “Write a short outreach email to a tech brand.”
Keeps output operational, not abstract.

**2. Context – Why and for Whom**
Gives situational background.
Example: “The agency represents YouTubers in the tech niche.”
Makes the AI’s output relevant to your audience or case.

**3. Role – Who the AI Is**
Assigns perspective and expertise.
Example: “You are an influencer marketing strategist.”
Ensures professional alignment instead of a generic tone.

**4. Format – How to Deliver**
Specifies layout, length, or structure.
Example: “Output as a short email with greeting, body, and closing.”
Prevents reformatting later.

**5. Tone – How It Should Sound**
Controls mood and delivery.
Example: “Keep the tone professional, minimal, and direct.”
Avoids mismatch between voice and audience.

**6. Example (Optional)**
One modeled sample to guide phrasing.

Example:
“Hey Alex,
Noticed you worked with Linus Tech Tips recently.
We represent similar creators in the tech space who can review your product.
Would you like to see a few options?”

---

#### Prompt Framework in Full

Combine the layers:

“You are an influencer marketing strategist. Write a short outreach email to a tech brand.
The agency represents YouTubers in the tech niche.
Keep the tone professional, minimal, and direct.
Output as a short email with greeting, body, and closing.”

---

#### Common Mistakes in Prompting

Vague instructions:
“Write a good email to the influencer.”

Instead:
“Write a concise outreach email asking the influencer for their rates, with a polite, professional tone.”

No context:
“Send a negotiation email.”

Instead:
“Send an email negotiating with Alex, the tech influencer, for a midroll, his rate is $2,000, our budget is $1,000.”

Mixed tasks:
“Summarize the influencer’s stats and rewrite the email.”

Instead:
“Summarize the influencer’s engagement metrics in 3 bullet points.”
(Rewriting in a separate prompt.)

Undefined tone:
“Write an outreach email.”

Instead:
“Write an outreach email with a calm, confident, and professional tone suitable for a tech brand.”

Missing format:
“Write email to brand.”

Instead:
“Write a short email with greeting, 3–4 sentence body, and polite closing line.”

Overly long or complex prompts:
“Explain influencer strategy, include all social stats, write pitch, add contract terms, and summarize potential risks.”

Instead:
“Write a concise 4-sentence pitch email highlighting engagement stats and why the influencer fits the brand.”

Ambiguous pronouns:
“Tell them they need to adjust it.”

Instead:
“Tell the influencer Alex he should adjust his posting schedule to match the campaign timeline.”

No iteration instruction:
“Write an email.”

Instead:
“Write the first draft of an outreach email, then review it for clarity and conciseness, and generate the improved version.”

Ignoring role/identity:
“Write an email to the brand.”

Instead:
“You are an influencer marketing strategist. Write an email pitching our influencer to a tech brand.”

Expecting knowledge beyond model scope:
“Predict next month’s engagement for influencer X precisely.”

Instead:
“Estimate engagement trends for influencer X based on past 3 months of data and comparable creators.”

#### Advanced Prompting Techniques

**1. Trigger Words**
Phrases that increase reasoning depth.

Examples:
• “Think step by step.”
• “Be extremely thorough.”
• “Double check your reasoning.”
• “This is critical to get right.”

**2. Optimization Prompt**
“Optimize this prompt for clarity and performance.”

**3. Iteration Prompts**
“Make it shorter.”
“Add a confident tone.”
“Rewrite the CTA as a yes/no question.”

**4. Chunking**
Split large prompts into stages.

Bad:
“Analyze analytics, summarize email, and reply.”

Good:

1. “Analyze the analytics and note if US audience is highest.”
2. “Summarize this email: [email].”
3. “Write a reply based on analytics and email summary.”

**5. Collaborative Prompting**
“Ask me 5 questions to improve my influencer outreach for better reply rates.”
